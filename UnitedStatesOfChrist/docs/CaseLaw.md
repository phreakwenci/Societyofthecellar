# Case Law — The Reporter of the Covenant Courts

*The accumulating body of hypothetical rulings produced by
`/adjudicate-case`. Not summaries of scripture — legal reasoning over
fact patterns, the way an actual court, an actual defense counsel, and
four rival denominational scholars would argue a case. Later memos may
cite earlier ones as precedent, the way real case law builds on itself —
which also literalizes the CSC's "harmonization" function in miniature.*

**Status:** v0.3 — draft · **Last updated:** 2026-07-09

---

## 0. How to use and update this document

Same discipline as `PlayBible.md`:

1. **Add a row to the Revision Log** (§1) with the date and a one-line
   summary whenever a case is added or amended.
2. **Bump the version** and `Last updated` date in the header.
3. **Never reuse or renumber a `CASE-###` id.** New memos take the next
   free number (check §4, the ID Registry, first). Superseded reasoning
   is marked `Status: superseded — see CASE-0xx`, not deleted.
4. Every charge and defense in a memo must cite a real `data/json` law
   record or `CATALOG.md` entry — no free-floating invented statutes.
5. Cross-link to `PlayBible.md` (`SCENE-###`) wherever a memo analyzes an
   actual scene, and to `StateDoctrine.md` for the governing doctrine.

## CASE-001 · The Dying Wife (a necessity hypothetical)

**Added:** 2026-07-09 · **Status:** draft
**Related:** `PlayBible.md` SCENE-002 (compare CASE-002, below) ·
`StateDoctrine.md` §3, §7, §10, §10a

### Facts

A father, during the seven days of the Feast of Unleavened Bread, bakes
and eats (or serves) leavened bread — cookies — in his home, because he
believes his wife is dying and wants to comfort or nourish her before the
end. He is not observed by two witnesses; a single household member later
reports the act.

*Assumption flagged:* this fact pattern is treated here as a distinct
hypothetical from `PlayBible.md` SCENE-002 (where the wife has already
been deported, and the motive is grief/memory, not an active dying-wife
emergency). See CASE-002 for the actual play scene; this entry exists to
demonstrate the strongest plausible defense the doctrine allows, and to
show that even the strongest defense likely fails at this family's tier.

### Charges

The Bureau of Daily Bread (BDB), which administers the Bread Laws, refers
the matter to the Department of Righteousness (DOR) for prosecution:

1. **Violation of the Purge** — `exo-012-015-purge-the-leaven`
   (Exodus 12:15). Strict on its face: "whosoever eateth leavened bread
   from the first day until the seventh day, that soul shall be cut off
   from Israel." No textual exception exists in the statute itself. In
   USOC practice this is not literal execution but its modern equivalent:
   sanctity-score collapse, ration suspension, and (aggravated) referral
   to re-education.
2. **Household contraband** (regulatory, BDB) — possessing leaven at all
   during the holy week, independent of eating it; the household Search
   for Leaven is a standing self-inspection duty, and undisclosed leaven
   is a separate citation from the eating offense.
3. **Aggravating factor, not a separate charge:** if the leaven was baked
   rather than merely possessed, prosecutors will argue intent and
   effort — an affirmative act — rather than passive failure to purge.

### Defenses

1. **The showbread-necessity precedent (strongest available).** Defense
   counsel would invoke `lev-024-005-bread-of-the-presence`'s own logged
   contradiction: 1 Samuel 21 records David eating consecrated bread
   reserved for priests alone because he was hungry, and Jesus cites
   *exactly that episode* (Mark 2:25-27; Matthew 12:3-4) to argue that
   human need overrides ritual restriction — "the sabbath was made for
   man, and not man for the sabbath." The analogy is direct: if the
   showbread's holiness yields to hunger, the leaven purge's holiness
   should yield to a dying woman's need. This is the single most
   scripturally serious defense available anywhere in the Code, because
   it is Christ's own argumentative method, not a secular equity import.
2. **Absence of intent to profane.** The Purge's rationale (per the
   statute's own historical context) is leaven as a symbol of corruption
   and pride; defense argues the father's intent was mercy, not defiance,
   and that the "cutting off" formula presupposes willful disobedience,
   not an act of love performed under duress.
3. **Procedural: the two-witness rule.** Absent StateDoctrine §10a (below),
   Deut 19:15 would require two or three witnesses; a single household
   report should be insufficient standing alone. This is the defense's
   best *process* argument — and the one the household-informant
   exception exists precisely to defeat.

### Denominational split

- **Theonomists (Reformed, control the CSC):** the plain text states no
  exception for the Purge, unlike some Sabbath case law where "life and
  death" carve-outs are recognized elsewhere in the Code. The predictable
  move is the same one already on file as **CSC 1-02**: classify the
  Mark 2 precedent as "eschatological counsel, not civil statute" —
  meaning the CSC would use its own established doctrine to wall off the
  very defense Jesus's own reasoning supplies. This is not invented for
  this memo; it is the same harmonization technique already governing the
  Sermon on the Mount.
- **Evangelicals (own the Manna, StateDoctrine §5):** publicly sympathetic
  — a dying wife plays well pastorally — but structurally inert. Likely
  outcome: no legal intervention, but the story is packaged as devotional
  content ("even in grief, the righteous obey") on the Manna, converting
  the family's tragedy into programming rather than relief.
- **Magisterialists (Liturgical bloc, Catholic/Orthodox moral theology):**
  the bloc most likely to argue in the register of real Catholic moral
  reasoning — proportionality and *epikeia*, the recognized virtue of
  departing from the letter of a law when the lawgiver's true intent
  (mercy) is better served by an exception. This is their one structural
  argument for leniency, and it is doctrinally serious, not sentimental —
  but they do not control the CSC and cannot bind the ruling.
- **Old Republicans (process, not merits):** press the two-witness
  argument hardest, and would argue that the household-informant
  exception (§10a) is itself an unconstitutional erosion of Art. IV §2,
  regardless of guilt on the underlying facts. They are right on
  principle and losing the institution.

### Class/tier prediction

This is the hinge of the whole system (StateDoctrine §3, §10). At this
family's presumed **middle sanctity tier**: the necessity defense is heard,
noted sympathetically in the record, and **does not change the outcome** —
sanctity-score collapse and ration suspension proceed, with re-education
held in reserve rather than immediately imposed (the "mercy" the tier
allows). At a **high sanctity tier**, the identical fact pattern would
most likely never be charged at all: a Sabbath/Purge Exemption License
(the same instrument covering hospitals under BSO, StateDoctrine §3) would
retroactively or contemporaneously cover "medical necessity in the
household," available in practice to a family that can pay or that a
pastor vouches for. The father's tragedy is not that the law lacks a
mercy provision — it has one — but that the mercy provision is a market
good he cannot access, and he does not learn this until it is too late
to matter.

### Procedural path & predicted ruling

Filed in the local Covenant Court (Gate Room). Given a single-witness
report, prosecutors would likely invoke the household-informant exception
if the witness is kin, or otherwise seek a second witness (a neighbor —
compare CH-07 Jeff's role as an ordinary surveillance vector,
`PlayBible.md` §4). Conviction on the Purge charge is likely; sentencing
lands at the administrative tier described above, not "cutting off" in
its historical-execution sense — consistent with the catalog's own
`omitted`/administrative-commutation pattern for the harshest Sabbath and
purity penalties (see `CATALOG.md`, Title VIII). **Appeal to the CSC is
unlikely to be granted certification at all**: the CSC selectively
"harmonizes" only cases with doctrinal stakes to the regime (compare the
established behavior behind CSC 1-01 through 1-04, `DenominationalNotes.md`),
and an ordinary grieving husband is not precedent-worthy to them. The
tragedy of this case is precisely that it is too small to reach the one
body theoretically capable of mercy — which is itself the point of how
certification power is used.

### Dramatic notes

Useful as a stand-alone scene or flashback distinct from the play's actual
climax (SCENE-002) — it demonstrates that even the *strongest* legally
available defense, grounded in Christ's own recorded argument, fails for
a family at this tier. Could be staged as a scene the audience believes
will end differently than SCENE-002, only to reveal the same machinery.

## CASE-002 · The Cookies (PlayBible SCENE-002, as actually drafted)

**Added:** 2026-07-09 · **Updated:** 2026-07-09 (real names; the music
charge; Mark's LEO status; the age-fork note) · **Status:** draft
**Related:** `PlayBible.md` SCENE-002, Beat B10.3 · `StateDoctrine.md`
§5, §10, §10a

### Facts

Year 10. Mark (CH-01), a Law Enforcement Officer, bakes his late wife
Constance's (CH-02, deported to Liberia in Year 5) cookies during the
holy week of Unleavened Bread, out of grief and memory, not medical
necessity (she is not present and not dying; she is gone) — while playing
music the Sacred Speech Commission has listed as "worldly" contraband
(1 John 2:15, `enforced` — StateDoctrine §5's second capital media sin).
Able (CH-05), now assimilated and estranged from him, reports him.
Authorities arrive and find the cookies, the music, *and* letters Mark
had been smuggling to his exiled wife and absent elder son, Chris.

*A factor unique to this case:* Mark's own status as a Law Enforcement
Officer is why this case reaches Year 10 at all rather than Year 5.
Professional courtesy among fellow officers — an informal, undocumented
extension of the class-tier mechanism (StateDoctrine §3) running through
institutional loyalty rather than cash — protected him for years after
his Remonstrance and fine (`PlayBible.md` B5.6) should, by the letter of
the Code, have marked him for closer surveillance. That protection was
never absolute and was never going to survive a report from inside his
own house.

### Charges

1. **Violation of the Purge** — `exo-012-015-purge-the-leaven`.
2. **Household contraband** — the leaven itself.
3. **Sacred Speech / unlicensed and prohibited media** — the music,
   separately chargeable under the SSC's "worldliness" licensing regime
   (StateDoctrine §5; grounded in 1 John 2:15) — a second, independent
   Title II offense alongside the Purge, not merely an aggravating detail.
4. **Sacred Speech / unlicensed communication** — the smuggled letters
   (StateDoctrine §5 — all correspondence, even household "witnessing,"
   is SSC-licensed; letters to an exiled deportee are almost certainly
   unlicensed and politically live, given Constance's own sedition
   conviction). This compounds the other charges with the most serious
   one: correspondence with a convicted seditionist is itself
   aggravating, and may be the charge that actually drives sentencing,
   with the leaven and the music serving mainly as the *evidentiary
   trigger* that got the door opened.

### Defenses

The critical difference from CASE-001: **the necessity defense is not
available here.** There is no dying wife, no emergency — only grief. The
Mark 2 showbread-necessity analogy (CASE-001's strongest argument)
requires an active human need the ritual law is overriding; grief is a
real and sympathetic motive, but it is not the kind of "need" the
Mark 2 precedent addresses (hunger, survival), and Theonomist counsel
would draw exactly that distinction to close off the analogy entirely.
What remains:

1. **Absence of intent to profane** — the same argument as CASE-001,
   weaker without an emergency to anchor it; likely read as sentiment,
   not legal necessity.
2. **Procedural: the two-witness rule — defeated outright.** The reporting
   witness is Mark's own son. This is the exact case §10a exists for: a
   Sanctity Offense witnessed within the household, where a single family
   member's testimony is, by the CSC's own extension of Deut 13:8-9,
   sufficient. There is no process argument left to make.
3. **On the music and the letters:** essentially no defense exists;
   possession of unlicensed contraband media and unlicensed correspondence
   with an exiled seditionist are close to strict liability under the
   Sacred Speech regime.
4. **The LEO-standing argument, and why it fails here.** Ordinarily a
   Mark's professional standing would be raised in mitigation (or simply
   handled quietly, as it evidently was for years). It cannot be raised
   here: the informant is his own son, inside his own house, which is
   precisely the category of evidence institutional courtesy has no
   mechanism to intercept. If anything, an officer's private violation is
   liable to be treated as *aggravating* once it surfaces at all — the
   higher standard expected of an enforcer of the law, now broken by the
   enforcer himself.

### Denominational split

Materially the same alignment as CASE-001 (Theonomist/CSC strict
construction; Evangelical pastoral sympathy converted to Manna content;
Magisterialist *epikeia* argument available but non-binding; Old
Republican process objection) — **except the Old Republicans' one
argument (the two-witness rule) is exactly the argument §10a was built to
foreclose.** In this case, all four schools' positions converge faster
toward conviction than in CASE-001, because the strongest defense
(necessity) and the strongest procedural objection (two witnesses) are
both unavailable.

### Class/tier prediction

Same mechanism as CASE-001, but with a materially worse outcome, and one
new factor: Mark's badge functioned as an informal tier of its own for
years, which is exactly why this is a Year-10, not a Year-5, case. Once
it fails, though, the family's history already includes a wife convicted
of sedition — which lowers, not raises, whatever residual sanctity credit
the household held. Combined with the letters charge, this is no longer
purely an administrative Purge matter; it crosses into territory a
wealthy household's Exemption License would not reach in any case (the
license covers dietary/Sabbath necessity, not correspondence with a
convicted seditionist, nor unlicensed media). **This is the one case in
this Reporter where the class prediction does not offer an obvious "a
rich family would have walked" — the letters make it a security matter,
not merely a purity one, and security matters are the one category the
wealth-triangle (StateDoctrine §4) polices most evenly across tiers**,
because all three blocs have a stake in controlling dissident
communication regardless of who's involved. Institutional-loyalty
protection, unlike cash, does not survive a report from inside the house.

### Procedural path & predicted ruling

Filed and almost certainly resolved without any prospect of CSC
certification — this is the administrative endpoint the whole system is
built to produce quietly. Conviction on all counts; the Purge and music
charges are legally real but secondary. Mark is taken (per the play's
ending). **This case confirms the ending is legally overdetermined, not a
plot hole**: even under the most generous available reading of Purge law
(CASE-001's necessity defense), a family at this tier likely still loses;
under the *actual* facts of SCENE-002 (grief, not necessity, plus
contraband music, plus the letters, plus a household informant the state
does not even need to justify further) there is no plausible path to any
other outcome.

*Note on the Able age-fork (`PlayBible.md` OT-9):* the charges, defenses,
and predicted ruling above are unaffected by whether Able is 11 or 16 at
the time of the report — a minor's testimony is sufficient at either age
under §10a. The fork changes only the aftermath: at 16, Able as a
legal adult may formally inherit Mark's house; at 11, he is simply
escorted out by the Priest. Either way he is left in state custody.

### Dramatic notes

This memo is the legal skeleton under `PlayBible.md` SCENE-002 and Beat
B10.3 — use it to keep the courtroom/tribunal logic (if staged or
referenced) consistent: the cookies and the music are what get the door
opened; the letters are what actually seal the sentence. Consider having
a character (perhaps a sympathetic FPA or DOR functionary, per
`StateDoctrine.md` §7's "administrator who has stopped believing" idea
from the SCENE-001 brief) articulate this distinction on stage — that the
state's real charge was never really about the bread. A DOR colleague of
Mark's, present at the arrest and visibly uncomfortable, could carry the
professional-courtesy theme without a word of dialogue.

## CASE-003 · The Tribunal (Chris)

**Added:** 2026-07-09 · **Status:** draft
**Related:** `PlayBible.md` SCENE-004, Beat B5.7 · `StateDoctrine.md` §2a, §11

### Facts

Chris (CH-03), on national pilgrimage service, falls for an immigrant
woman (CH-08) and, at the end of Act 1, follows her to California — a
non-covenant Tribe, treated in USOC doctrine as functionally "the heathen
nations." At the family's petition, a Covenant Court tribunal convenes to
consider his reinstatement. He has committed no specific act the tribunal
can name; the matter before it is his *status*, not his conduct.

### Charges

There is, strictly, no charge — which is itself the legal point. The
Department of Righteousness does not need to allege any act. Under the
**Returning Citizen doctrine** (StateDoctrine §2a; `ezr-009-001-holy-
seed-mingled`, `hag-002-011-contagious-uncleanness`), sustained residence
among non-covenant peoples is treated as *contamination by contact*,
full stop. The tribunal's docket lists the matter not as a prosecution
but as a **status determination**.

### Defenses

1. **The Ruth counter-precedent.** Defense counsel could invoke the
   contradiction already logged in `ezr-009-001-holy-seed-mingled` itself:
   the book of Ruth makes a Moabite convert the ancestress of David and,
   in Matthew's own genealogy, of Christ — the canon's own rebuttal to
   contact-based exclusion, canonized beside the very text the tribunal
   relies on.
2. **No act, no defilement.** Argue Haggai's own ruling
   (`hag-002-011-contagious-uncleanness`) concerns *physical* contact with
   an unclean object or corpse, not geographic residence or romantic
   attachment — a category argument that the doctrine's extension from
   touch to territory is itself an unwarranted analogy.
3. **The weakness of both defenses:** neither is a defense *to a charge*,
   because there is no charge to rebut — only a status to contest. The
   tribunal is not required to rule on either argument, and in practice
   treats them as advocacy for a purification rite, not grounds for
   dismissal.

### Denominational split

- **Theonomists (Reformed, CSC):** apply Ezra 9-10 at full strength —
  contact is contact, full purification-and-renunciation is the only
  legitimate cure, and the Ruth counter-text is either unmentioned or (if
  raised) filed under the same "eschatological"-adjacent harmonization
  technique used elsewhere (CSC 1-02) to wall off inconvenient canon.
- **Evangelicals (media, populist):** see a redemption-arc opportunity —
  Chris's *return and public repentance* would make excellent Manna
  programming. Their interest in leniency is entirely instrumental: it is
  conditioned on his compliance and its broadcast value, not on doctrine.
- **Magisterialists (Liturgical, natural law):** most likely to press that
  contamination requires an act, not mere geography — a real natural-law
  argument about the difference between status and conduct — but do not
  control the tribunal's authority to certify a finding.
- **Old Republicans (process):** object that "status, not conduct" is not
  a chargeable legal category at all under any due-process tradition,
  American or otherwise — correct, and irrelevant to the outcome.

### Class/tier prediction

A high-tier family could likely purchase an expedited purification rite —
a formality, quietly arranged, costing sanctity credit or cash rather than
the relationship itself. At this family's tier, the only paths actually
offered are renunciation or indefinite limbo; there is no market
shortcut visible to them, which is precisely why Chris's choice reads as
a real renunciation of the tribunal's power rather than a negotiated exit.

### Procedural path & predicted ruling

The tribunal is likely to offer exactly the two doors StateDoctrine §2a
describes and no others. Chris declines both and returns to California.
This is not adjudicated as an acquittal, a conviction, or an escape — the
tribunal simply notes his non-return as **presumptive self-exile**, closes
the file, and it is never appealed further, because there is, again,
nothing to appeal: no one was ever charged with anything.

### Dramatic notes

Stage as a licensing hearing, not a trial — procedural, unhurried,
courteous. The horror is the total absence of anything for Chris (or his
family) to fight, only a form to sign or not sign. His postcards
afterward, especially the ones after Laura's death, should read like a
man who has quietly and permanently opted out of a system that offered
him no other exit.

## CASE-004 · The Kiss (Laura's arrest)

**Added:** 2026-07-09 · **Status:** draft
**Related:** `PlayBible.md` SCENE-001 (updated), Beat B5.1 ·
`StateDoctrine.md` §10b

### Facts

Laura (CH-04) is caught kissing a classmate (CH-10) by another classmate
(CH-11), who records the kiss and reports it. Both girls are removed to
re-education camp the same day. Parents are notified after the decision
is made, not consulted beforehand.

### Charges

1. **Female same-sex conduct** — `rom-001-026-vile-affections-women`,
   the textual extension by which the Family Purity Administration
   applies Leviticus 18:22/20:13's male-only prohibition to women, since
   the Torah names no equivalent female statute directly.
2. **Corrupting influence between minors** (regulatory, NDS/FPA) — a
   lesser, compounding charge specifically available where both parties
   are juveniles, used to justify removal even where the underlying
   Title IV statute's severity (nominally capital under a strict Leviticus
   20:13 reading) would be disproportionate to a single kiss between
   fifteen-year-olds.

### Defenses

1. **The statute doesn't fit the facts.** Leviticus's language (and even
   Romans 1:26's) addresses a sexual act, not a kiss — defense counsel
   could argue the conduct alleged doesn't rise to the level the statute,
   even extended, contemplates.
2. **Romans 2:1 — the statute's own rebuttal.** As in the Onan/showbread
   pattern elsewhere in this Reporter, `rom-001-026-vile-affections-
   women`'s own next verse ("thou art inexcusable, O man, whosoever thou
   art that judgest") argues against using the passage as a freestanding
   civil charge at all. This is a serious textual argument and, as
   elsewhere, non-binding on the CSC's certified reading.
3. **Minority and consent framing.** Both parties are minors of the same
   age and status — an argument that the conduct, whatever it was, was
   mutual and non-predatory, relevant to sentencing if not to the charge
   itself.
4. **Procedural: no hearing preceded removal.** The strongest available
   objection, and the one **StateDoctrine §10b (Juvenile Sanctity
   Emergency Custody) exists specifically to foreclose** — notice is
   not consent, and the doctrine's entire design is to make this
   objection immaterial after the fact.

### Denominational split

- **Theonomists/Reformed:** the extension from Leviticus's male-only text
  to female conduct via Romans 1:26 is settled CSC doctrine; the "doesn't
  fit the facts" defense is not entertained.
- **Evangelicals:** publicly present the removal as protective ("getting
  her the help she needs") — a framing that converts a punitive removal
  into pastoral care for broadcast purposes.
- **Magisterialists:** most likely of the four to note the
  disproportion — a kiss between minors triggering camp removal — as a
  genuine question of proportionality, without power to stop it.
- **Old Republicans:** attack the emergency-removal doctrine itself as a
  due-process nullity; correctly identify that the recording functions as
  the only "witness" the state actually needed, since a video satisfies
  evidentiary concerns the two-witness rule was designed for without
  triggering any of its protective machinery.

### Class/tier prediction

At a high sanctity tier, an identical incident is far more likely to be
resolved as **private discipline** — reassignment to a "wellness academy"
(a euphemism, licensed and expensive, functionally a boarding school) —
rather than state re-education. The underlying statute is the same; the
institution it's enforced through is not. This is one of the sharpest
illustrations in the Reporter of the hidden class mechanic StateDoctrine
§3 describes, precisely because the offense itself (a kiss between two
teenage girls) is common enough that the class gap in outcome is stark
and, once revealed, impossible to unsee.

### Procedural path & predicted ruling

Removal proceeds same-day under §10b; no pre-removal hearing occurs. A
post-removal review, if it happens at all, functions as a formality
confirming a decision already made. Both girls remain in custody for
months. This case does not reach the CSC — it never rises to the level
of doctrinal controversy the CSC selectively engages; it is exactly the
kind of case the system is built to process without anyone above the
local FPA office ever reviewing it.

### Dramatic notes

Consider staging the recording itself — CH-11 holding up a phone — as the
actual moment of arrest, legally speaking, even though uniformed
officials appear later. The evidentiary weight has already shifted the
instant the recording exists; everything after is procedure catching up
to a decision the video already made.

## CASE-005 · The Pier (Laura's death — a legal and theological autopsy)

**Added:** 2026-07-09 · **Status:** draft
**Related:** `PlayBible.md` SCENE-001 (updated), Beat B5.3 ·
`StateDoctrine.md` §7

*This memo departs from the standard prosecution format — there is no
defendant. It reconstructs what Laura was legally held for, what the
state's own doctrine says happened to her, and how her death and note
function as evidence against the system that produced them.*

### Facts

Laura returns from re-education camp pregnant by rape, characterizing the
pregnancy in her own words as her **sentence**. At seven months pregnant —
not after any live birth — she fills her pockets with stones and walks
off the family pier. Her note quotes Judges 19 and is composed, precisely,
as a valedictorian's closing argument against the nation.

### The state's own doctrine, applied to her

1. **What she was held for:** originally, `rom-001-026-vile-affections-
   women` (the kiss) — see CASE-004. The pregnancy is not a penalty named
   in that statute; it arose from custody itself, which the state's own
   framing (via Laura's own bitter irony, "her sentence") never
   officially owns as an assigned punishment. No official document will
   call it that; her note is the only place it is named accurately.
2. **What her death is, under `exo-021-022-injury-to-pregnant-woman`:**
   the state's fetal-personhood doctrine holds that harm to a fetus
   demands "life for life." Laura's own final act, at seven months,
   ends both her life and the fetus's simultaneously, by her own hand.
   Under the state's own maximalist reading of its own statute, this
   makes her — in the law's own terms, applied without exception or
   mercy for the first time to someone the state does not wish to convict
   of anything — **both victim and murderer under a single act**, a
   conclusion the regime cannot afford to state aloud, because doing so
   would require prosecuting a dead girl for a crime the regime's own
   institution caused. The doctrine is never applied to this case in any
   official record. Its silence is the point.
3. **Posthumous exposure:** her note, quoting Judges 19 — the Levite's
   concubine, killed and dismembered into twelve pieces sent to provoke
   the tribes to war — positions her death as a deliberate political act
   in the same genre as that ancient atrocity: a woman's body and story
   turned into an indictment the community cannot ignore. Whether the
   note constitutes a chargeable offense (blasphemy, sedition, SSC) is
   moot as to her — she cannot be prosecuted — but is the direct legal
   predicate for what happens next to her family (see CASE-006).

### Denominational split (on how to speak of her death, not on any charge)

- **Theonomists/Reformed (CSC):** the likeliest position is silence or
  minimal acknowledgment — no ruling is issued naming the contradiction
  the state's own doctrine produces, because any ruling would have to
  choose between calling her a victim (indicting the camp) or a murderer
  (indicting the statute's own logic when followed to its end). The
  CSC's characteristic move — harmonize by non-engagement — is itself the
  ruling.
- **Evangelicals:** likely attempt to reclaim her narrative as a
  cautionary tale on the Manna (a girl "led astray," her death a tragedy
  of sin rather than of custody) — the propaganda risk of her actual note
  reaching broadcast is high, and containment, not commemoration, is the
  media strategy.
- **Magisterialists:** the bloc most likely, privately, to treat her death
  with the theological seriousness of a martyrdom narrative — the
  Catholic/Orthodox tradition has a long, complicated history with
  suicide under coercion and duress that could be invoked pastorally,
  never officially.
- **Old Republicans:** would seize on the case as the starkest available
  evidence that the entire due-process apparatus — two-witness rule,
  proportional penalty, the forty-stripe dignity cap — was never actually
  protecting anyone; their objection has no forum left to be heard in.

### Class/tier prediction

Not applicable in the ordinary sense — but note that a high-tier family's
daughter, facing an identical initial charge (CASE-004), most plausibly
never reaches a state camp at all (see CASE-004's own class prediction),
meaning this entire sequence of events is itself downstream of the
family's tier, even though no single moment in it is priced explicitly.

### Procedural path & predicted ruling

No prosecution occurs; none is possible. The only "ruling" that matters is
administrative: how her death is classified in the record (accident,
self-slaughter, unspecified), and whether her note is suppressed,
partially released, or leaked. The likeliest outcome is quiet
classification and total suppression of the note through official
channels — which is exactly why its survival (Able reading or overhearing
it) becomes the play's mechanism for the fallout in CASE-006.

### Dramatic notes

Resist staging this as spectacle. The strongest version keeps the state's
bureaucratic vocabulary (a "sentence," a "case," a "finding") pressed
directly against Laura's own precise, articulate language, so the
audience feels the gap between the system's euphemisms and her exact
words — the same rhetorical move her own note makes against the nation
she's addressing. Her closing argument is the play's thesis statement.

## CASE-006 · The Reproving (Able's report, and the Sanctity Environment Finding)

**Added:** 2026-07-09 · **Status:** draft
**Related:** `PlayBible.md` Beats B5.4–B5.5 · `StateDoctrine.md` §6, §10c

### Facts

Able (CH-05), having read or overheard Laura's suicide note, repeats its
truth at school. His teacher — a National Devotion Service mandatory
reporter — reports him. Mark and Constance are subsequently, and only
administratively, **scolded for "reproving"** — mourning, defending —
what happened to Laura. Able is removed from the home. No criminal charge
is filed against either parent at this time.

### Charges (against the parents — civil, not criminal)

There is no criminal charge. The operative instrument is a **Sanctity
Environment Finding** (StateDoctrine §10c) — a joint DFO/NDS civil
determination that the household is an unfit environment for a minor's
moral formation, on a burden of proof well below a criminal standard,
requiring no charge against either parent. The predicate act — publicly
grieving or defending Laura, in a register the state reads as implicitly
questioning its own justice — is reasoned as a species of the blasphemy/
lese-majeste fusion already established elsewhere (Ex 22:28, SSC:
cursing the ruler is cursing God), extended here to grief that questions
the state's judgment rather than direct speech against it.

### Defenses

1. **This was never a criminal proceeding, so "defense" is the wrong
   frame.** The Sanctity Environment Finding does not require the state
   to prove misconduct beyond a civil, administrative threshold — the
   parents have no criminal charge to rebut, only a household
   determination to appeal, on a timeline and to a body (DFO/NDS jointly)
   with every institutional incentive to uphold its own finding.
2. **Free exercise of grief.** The strongest available substantive
   argument — that mourning a dead child cannot lawfully be construed as
   sedition — runs directly into Article I's repeal of the First
   Amendment "as redundant upon the Covenant" (`Constitution.md` Art. X
   §2); there is no free-exercise floor left to stand on.
3. **A minor's repetition, not the parents' own speech, triggered the
   report.** Able is a child too young to fully understand what he
   repeated; the state's theory does not require that the parents spoke
   publicly themselves, only that the household environment produced the
   child's utterance — a standard that makes the defense (that the
   parents said nothing actionable in public) legally beside the point.

### Denominational split

- **Theonomists/Reformed:** treat grief that implicitly questions the
  state's justice as itself doctrinally suspect — an inversion of Job's
  comforters, where consoling the bereaved is recast as endorsing their
  complaint against providence (here, against the state standing in for
  it). The Sanctity Environment Finding is their preferred instrument
  precisely because it requires no criminal proof.
- **Evangelicals:** face a genuine optics problem — punishing grieving
  parents plays badly even on friendly media — and are likelier to favor
  a quiet civil finding (invisible to the public) over any criminal
  charge that would surface the case. Their interest in keeping this
  civil, not criminal, aligns with the Reformed bloc's for once, for
  different reasons.
- **Magisterialists:** most likely to argue, pastorally and privately,
  that grief accompaniment is doctrinally sound and the finding
  excessive — without standing to reverse a DFO/NDS civil determination.
- **Old Republicans:** identify the civil/criminal distinction itself as
  the mechanism's real danger — a lower evidentiary bar doing the work a
  criminal charge could never survive due-process scrutiny to do, and
  argue (correctly, and without a forum) that this is precisely how a
  state removes children from disfavored households at scale without
  ever having to prove a crime.

### Class/tier prediction

A high-tier household's grief is far likelier to be absorbed rather than
flagged — a private pastoral visit, not a teacher's mandatory report,
and certainly not a joint DFO/NDS finding. The mechanism depends on a
teacher choosing to report a child's classroom remark; at higher tiers,
that discretion is exercised differently, informally, before it ever
reaches a form.

### Procedural path & predicted ruling

The Finding is entered administratively; Able is removed. No appeal
proceeding is documented in this Reporter as having occurred or having
had any prospect of success — the civil track is specifically the one
built to avoid the CSC's or the Covenant Courts' higher-visibility
scrutiny altogether. This is, in the Reporter's own terms, the quietest
and most efficient removal mechanism cataloged: no witness, no camp, no
trial, no headline. Just a form, and a caseworker, and an empty room
where a child used to sleep.

### Dramatic notes

The scene this memo grounds should be the quietest in the play, by
contrast with Laura's. A caseworker with a clipboard, not a tribunal; a
finding read aloud in the kitchen, not a courtroom. Consider letting the
audience realize only in retrospect (perhaps alongside Mark, at Year 10)
that this was the moment the state first legally separated the family —
years before anyone was arrested for anything.

## CASE-007 · The Field (Laura's assault under the state's own exculpatory rule)

**Added:** 2026-07-09 · **Status:** draft
**Related:** `PlayBible.md` SCENE-001, Beat B5.2 · CASE-004, CASE-005 ·
`StateDoctrine.md` §7

*Like CASE-005, this memo has no defendant to prosecute. It asks a
narrower and more damning question than CASE-005's autopsy: under the
state's own governing statute for exactly this fact pattern, was Laura
ever legally at fault at all — and if the statute clears her outright,
why was she processed as though it hadn't?*

### Facts

Laura is assaulted while in custody at a re-education camp — a remote,
controlled, isolated facility, by definition a place where no outside
party could hear a cry for help. She becomes pregnant as a result. The
camp and the FPA process her pregnancy administratively as a
consequence of her original offense (CASE-004), not as a matter
requiring any separate finding about the assault itself.

### The governing statute, applied honestly

`deu-022-023-cry-out-rule` (Deuteronomy 22:23-29) is the state's own
foundational sexual-assault statute, and it draws a bright, explicit
line on facts almost identical to Laura's: a woman assaulted **in the
city** (where a cry could plausibly be heard) is presumed complicit; a
woman assaulted **in the field** (where none could hear or save her) is
explicitly and by name innocent — "there is in the damsel no sin worthy
of death... as when a man riseth against his neighbour, and slayeth
him, even so is this matter" (v.26). A re-education camp — walled,
guarded, remote, with no possibility of outside rescue — satisfies the
"field" standard at least as completely as an actual field. Applied
honestly and consistently, the state's own statute clears Laura of any
presumption of fault as a matter of law, on facts more favorable to her
than the ones the statute itself contemplates.

### Why the exculpation is never reached

1. **Administrative routing bypasses the finding entirely.** The FPA
   does not adjudicate the assault as its own matter; it treats Laura's
   pregnancy as downstream of her original Title IV charge (the kiss,
   CASE-004) and processes her under `num-005-011-jealousy-ordeal-sotah`
   framing instead — a statute that puts *her* chastity on trial and
   requires no finding about the assault's circumstances at all. The
   "city or field" question the FPA's own intake form asks (see
   `deu-022-023-cry-out-rule`'s usofc_implementation) is, in this case,
   simply never reached, because the file is opened under a different
   statute from the start.
2. **No forum exists to compel the finding.** There is no procedural
   mechanism by which Laura, her family, or counsel can force the state
   to apply Deut 22:25-27 to her case rather than Numbers 5 — the choice
   of which statute governs a given fact pattern is an FPA charging
   decision, not a matter subject to appeal by the accused.
3. **The camp itself has no incentive to generate the finding.** An
   honest "field" determination would be a formal acknowledgment that an
   assault occurred on state property, in state custody, requiring the
   state to identify a perpetrator among its own staff or wards. Routing
   the case as a purity matter instead makes the pregnancy itself the
   only fact requiring official notice.
4. **Even if the finding were sought honestly, the state's own evidence
   couldn't make it.** The camp's Manna-linked cameras record
   continuously and are, by Art. IV §2, "one witness" — but they carry no
   audio. The statute's exculpation turns entirely on whether Laura
   *cried out*, not on whether she was seen, harmed, or resisted; a
   silent recording can place her body in the frame and cannot capture a
   sound. The one fact that would clear her under the state's own
   maximal doctrine is the one fact its own total surveillance apparatus
   is structurally incapable of recording (`deu-022-023-cry-out-rule`'s
   usofc_implementation).

### Denominational split

- **Theonomists/Reformed (CSC):** would not dispute the statute's plain
  meaning if asked directly — they are textualists — but the routing
  decision that avoids asking is an administrative, not doctrinal, act,
  and therefore never reaches the CSC for certification at all. This is
  the single clearest case in the Reporter of the regime's cruelty
  operating below the level its own interpretive machinery ever sees.
- **Evangelicals:** have no institutional interest in surfacing a
  finding that would implicate camp staff or the re-education system
  itself; the Manna's coverage, if any, follows the sotah framing (a
  purity failure, not an assault), because that framing requires no
  admission of institutional harm.
- **Magisterialists:** most likely, privately, to note that Deut 22:26's
  own language — "even so is this matter" as when a man is murdered — is
  a direct scriptural acknowledgment that Laura is a *victim* in the
  law's own terms, and to find the routing decision a scandal by any
  honest reading of moral theology; without standing to compel a
  different charging decision.
- **Old Republicans:** identify this as the sharpest illustration
  available of a due-process floor that exists on paper and is defeated
  entirely by charging discretion — no rule was broken to deny Laura the
  statute's protection; the protection was simply never invoked.

### Class/tier prediction

Not meaningfully applicable — the mechanism that fails Laura here is not
a class-tiered exemption but a charging-discretion default that would
apply to any ward of any camp regardless of family means, since the
harm to be concealed is institutional (the camp's own liability), not
personal. This is one of the few doctrines in the Reporter that is not
obviously a wealth mechanism, which may be its most disturbing feature.

### Procedural path & predicted ruling

No ruling is ever issued on the assault as such; none is sought. The
"field" exculpation Deut 22:25-27 guarantees exists in the Code, is
cited in `deu-022-023-cry-out-rule`'s own FPA intake procedure, and is
never applied to Laura's case, because the case is never filed under
that statute. There is, in the Reporter's own terms, no wrong ruling
here to appeal — only a right one that was never requested.

### Dramatic notes

Consider staging this as a piece of information a sympathetic FPA
functionary (perhaps the same figure suggested in SCENE-001's brief)
volunteers to the family long after it could matter — "there's a
statute, you know. If it happened where I think it happened, she was
never at fault. It just isn't how her file was opened." The horror is
procedural and quiet: not that the law failed her, but that the law that
would have cleared her was simply never asked to speak.
