# The Moscow Model — the origin case study

*How one town proved the method a decade before there was a nation to
apply it to. Companion to `ConstitutionalHistory.md` (the national
timeline, which this doc precedes) and `StateDoctrine.md` (the
governance this method eventually became).*

---

## A note on how this is built, read this before anything else

This case study is set in a real place — Moscow, Idaho, a small college
town in the real Palouse wheat country of the Idaho Panhandle, near the
Washington border. That town, in reality, is the most visible home in
America right now of a real, public, and extensively self-published
theological and political movement: Christian Reconstructionism and its
descendants, arguing openly for postmillennial "dominion," classical
Christian education as a multi-generational strategy, and a patriarchal
household theology — the same real intellectual lineage (R. J.
Rushdoony's actual writings) already cited as the historical father of
this project's fictional "Theonomist" bloc since `ConstitutionalModel.md`
was first drafted.

**The choice made here, deliberately: the town is real; the church, its
college, its press, and its founding pastor are not.** No living person
is named or depicted, and no real institution is claimed to have done
anything in this document. What is extrapolated — accurately, and from
publicly reported, widely documented tactics associated with this real
movement's actual, real-world Moscow presence (systematic acquisition of
downtown commercial property, a classical Christian school and college,
an aggressive publishing and media operation, sustained campaigns for
local office) — is taken to its worst-case, most complete conclusion by
a wholly invented institution. This is satire of a real, public,
self-described strategy, not a depiction of any named individual's
private conduct or of any fictional crime attached to a real name. If
the intent is ever to go further than this — to name and depict real,
living people directly — that is a different, much riskier document, and
should be a deliberate choice made in full awareness of what it costs,
not a default.

## 1. The town, before

Moscow, Idaho: a small university town (the real University of Idaho
sits there), a farming county seat in wheat and lentil country, ordinary
in almost every visible way. Nothing about its geography or its economy
marked it out. What marked it out was that a small, serious, well-funded,
and extremely patient congregation decided, across roughly a generation,
that a college town of modest size was more capturable than a city — and
they were right.

## 2. The institutions (fictional)

- **Covenant Kirk of the Palouse** — a Reformed congregation, founded
  decades before the national story begins, doctrinally postmillennial:
  the conviction, genuinely held and genuinely argued in print, that
  Christians are called to progressively bring every institution of
  society under Christ's rule *before* his return, not to wait passively
  for it. This is not a caricature; it is a real, coherent, publicly
  defended theological position in the real movement this is drawn from,
  and the Kirk's fictional founder preached it exactly as written.
- **Pastor Josiah Renner** — the Kirk's founding pastor, a fictional
  figure. Prolific, media-savvy, unbothered by controversy and often
  visibly energized by it; his sermons, later his books, argued for
  male headship, classical education, and postmillennial dominion in
  the same confident, plainspoken register that makes the real
  movement's actual literature so widely read outside its own
  congregations.
- **Ezra College** — named, in-world, for the returned exile who purged
  a mixed community back to covenant purity (`ezr-009-001-holy-seed-
  mingled` — the name is not an accident the college's founders were
  shy about). A classical Christian college, founded small, explicitly
  to train "a generation of magistrates" — the real, stated rationale of
  the classical-Christian-education movement this is drawn from, which
  argues openly that education is the multi-generational lever, not a
  side project.
- **Threshing Floor Press** — the Kirk's publishing arm. Prolific, cheap,
  widely distributed, deliberately unacademic in tone; its real-world
  counterpart is one of the most effective tools this movement actually
  has, because it makes the ideology exportable in a way a single
  congregation never could be.

## 3. The method: capture, not conquest

Nothing about what follows required breaking a law that existed at the
time. That is the entire point, and it is drawn accurately from a real,
reported, and largely legal playbook:

1. **Property first.** Over roughly fifteen years, the Kirk and its
   members' businesses acquired a steadily growing share of Moscow's
   own downtown commercial real estate — storefronts, a print shop, a
   coffeehouse, event space — not seized, bought, at market rates, one
   listing at a time, faster than anyone not counting noticed.
2. **Institutions second.** Ezra College grew from a handful of
   students meeting in borrowed rooms to an accredited, then a
   flagship, institution — and its graduates, trained explicitly to
   understand civil office as a Christian calling, began quietly filling
   the seats a small town always has too few candidates for: the
   library board, the planning commission, eventually the school board
   and the city council itself, seats no one else particularly wanted
   until it was too late to want them back.
3. **Publication third.** Threshing Floor Press turned the Kirk's local
   theology into a portable, replicable doctrine — sermons and manuals
   any congregation anywhere could pick up and apply. The Press did not
   sell Moscow. It sold the *method*.
4. **Patience, always.** No election was ever stolen. No ordinance was
   ever passed that the town's own charter didn't allow. The Kirk's
   defenders, when the town's newspapers finally noticed what had
   happened, had an easy and largely true answer: everything was done
   in the open, and anyone could have run against them and didn't.

By the time Moscow was widely discussed elsewhere as a case study — a
small American town that had, functionally, been reoriented around a
single congregation's institutions and doctrine without a single
illegal act along the way — the Kirk had already stopped being a local
story.

## 4. The spread: a network before there was a nation

Threshing Floor Press's manuals did their work. Over the following
decade, a loose, informal network of "Kirk towns" — the term the
national press eventually settled on, never a name the network gave
itself — appeared across the rural interior West: small county seats
in Idaho, Wyoming, Montana, the Dakotas, and the inland counties of
Oregon and Washington, each running some local variant of the same
three-part method (property, institutions, publication), each in
contact with the others through the Press's conferences and the
Kirk's own visiting-pastor circuit.

No law bound these towns together. No government existed yet to bind
them. What existed was a demonstration, repeated often enough to stop
looking like a coincidence: **this works, and it works within the rules
already on the books.**

## 5. From method to nation

This is the piece `ConstitutionalHistory.md`'s "Before Year 0" section
does not yet say plainly enough, and should: the Covenant Referendum's
winning argument was never purely rhetorical. **"Take Back the Nation
for Christ" was sold, town by town, with an answer to the obvious
question — has anyone ever actually done this? — and the answer, for
a decade before the vote, was yes, look at the Palouse.** The Leave
campaign's most effective literature was not scripture or slogans; it
was a map of the Kirk-town network, already governing itself, already
proof that the abstract proposal was a completed local fact in dozens
of places before it was ever a national one.

And the **Covenant Admission Act** (`FederalCode.md` Title I §5.1;
`Constitution.md` Art. V §4) — the two-thirds, county-by-county
mechanism that quietly annexes willing rural counties after Year 0 — is
not a new invention of the national government. It is the Kirk-town
method, formalized into statute: property and institutions and patient
legal capture, now simply given a certificate at the end of it. The
national Constitution did not invent the playbook. It licensed one
already running.

## 6. Moscow's status after Year 0

Latah County — Moscow's county — requires no admission under the
Covenant Admission Act; it is part of Idaho, a founding Tribe, from
Year 0. But its status is not ordinary. Ezra College becomes, within
the first several years, the *de facto* national seminary — the
institution that trains a disproportionate share of the clergy who
staff the Department of Worship, the National Devotion Service, and,
in time, the Committee on Scriptural Consistency itself. Moscow is
spoken of, in state media and in ordinary conversation alike, as
**the Cradle** — the place it was first proven, before it was a country.
This is not a formal constitutional designation; it needs none. Everyone
already knows what the town is.

*Drives (`PlayBible.md`):* a family living in or near Moscow is not
living in an ordinary Tribe. They are living at the model town, under
the specific, exhausting scrutiny of a community that has spent longer
than almost anywhere else perfecting the performance of sincere,
observed devotion — and under a Kirk whose founding pastor's
descendants and students, not the pastor himself, now largely run both
the college and, quietly, the county.
