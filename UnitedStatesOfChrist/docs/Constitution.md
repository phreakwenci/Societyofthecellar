# The Constitution of the United States of Christ

*As ratified by the Covenant Convention and certified consonant with
Scripture by the First Council of Overseers. Working draft for the
fiction — every clause cites its scriptural warrant, and every warrant
is in the Catalog (`data/catalog/CATALOG.md`).*

*This is the **Year-0 artifact**: the founding compromise text as
ratified, preserved unchanged. What constitutional traditions it is built
from — American/Confederate form, Iranian clerical machinery, Puritan
franchise, and a Brexit-style referendum origin hardening into Hungarian
capture — is analyzed in `ConstitutionalModel.md`. How its meaning drifts
over the first decade without a word of the text changing is traced in
`ConstitutionalHistory.md`.*

*A note on what this document is not. This Constitution defines
**institutions and powers** — who may do what, and by what process. It
does not enumerate crimes, penalties, or the daily obligations of
citizens; those are the province of the **Covenant Code** (the Titles
catalogued in `CATALOG.md` and drafted section by section in
`FederalCode.md`). Nor does it show how these powers are actually
exercised on the ground; that is the province of **Case Law**
(`CaseLaw.md`). A citizen who wants to know what the government *is*
reads this. A citizen who wants to know what is *forbidden* reads the
Code. A citizen who wants to know what actually *happens* reads the
cases — and learns, generally, that the three documents agree less than
they should.*

*A note on register (added after review): this text is written the way a
real legislature would actually write and vote on it — plain, direct,
legally functional language, with Scripture cited as authority rather
than used as the grammar of the law itself. A room of senators has to be
able to read a bill and know what it does. The satire lives in what the
government does with plain power, not in how hard the document is to
parse. Direct scriptural quotation is kept short, and used where a law
is genuinely quoting its source, not paraphrasing around it. The heavier
editorial commentary formerly interleaved through each Article — the
jokes, the asides, the dramatic ironies — has been moved to a single
closing note ("For the reader," at the end) or to the companion docs
that already carry this material in more developed form
(`ConstitutionalHistory.md`, `DenominationalNotes.md`, `Agencies.md`,
`StateDoctrine.md`). Nothing substantive was cut — only relocated or
folded into the operative text as plain fact rather than aside.*

## Preamble

We the People of the United States, having been as sheep going astray,
and being now returned unto the Shepherd and Bishop of our souls, in
order to form a more perfect Communion, establish Righteousness, insure
domestic Purity, provide for the common Defence of the Faith, promote
the general Sanctification, and secure the Blessings of Obedience to
ourselves and our Posterity unto the third and fourth generation, do
ordain and establish this Constitution for the United States of Christ.

---

## Article I — The Covenant

**Section 1. Supremacy.** The Bible is the supreme law of the United
States of Christ. Wherever this Constitution, or the constitution or
laws of any Tribe, conflicts with it, the Bible governs. This principle
may not be amended away (Deut 4:2).

**Section 2. Construction.** Where the Bible speaks directly to a
question, the courts must follow it (Deut 17:11). Where the Bible is
silent, the Council of Overseers decides what it would have said. Where
two passages conflict, the Committee on Scriptural Consistency issues a
harmonizing ruling, and that ruling is treated as the Bible's original
meaning.

**Section 3. The Twelve Titles.** The Covenant Code is organized into
twelve Titles, one for each tribe of Israel: the Covenant; Worship and
Devotion; Family Order; Persons and Violence; Servitude and Labor;
Property and Restitution; Purity and Diet; Festivals and the Calendar;
Justice and Procedure; Revenue; Aliens and Strangers; and War. The
Catalog assigns all 613 commandments of the Law and 109 norms of the New
Covenant among these Titles.

**Section 4. The Epigraph.** Every copy of this Constitution and of the
Code must print this verse on its title page: *"I have set before you
life and death, blessing and cursing: therefore choose life"* (Deut
30:19).

---

## Article II — The Presidency of the Covenant

**Section 1. Vesting.** Executive power belongs to a President of the
Covenant. The President must be a communicant certified by the Council,
and a citizen by birth, not a foreigner (Deut 17:15). The term of office
is set by the Code.

**Section 2. The King's Copy.** Before taking office, the President must
personally write out a copy of the Law and read from it every day of his
term, so that he does not think himself above other citizens (Deut
17:18-20). This copy must be made public. Whether he actually reads it
is not something this Constitution can verify.

**Section 3. Appointments.** The President nominates, and the Council
confirms, the judges of the Covenant Courts and the heads of the
enforcement departments — Righteousness, Restitution, Servitude
Oversight, Revenue, Daily Bread, Agricultural Holiness, and Ritual
Cleanliness among them. The Council appoints the doctrinal departments
directly, without a presidential nomination: Worship, Idolatry
Prevention, Sacred Speech, the Committee on Scriptural Consistency, and
the Devotion Service. In short, the President runs the agencies that
enforce the Law; the Council alone decides what the Law means, and who
is authorized to teach it.

**Section 4. Command.** The President commands the Ministry of Covenant
Defense and the militia of the Tribes when called into national service,
subject to Title XII and to any finding of war or peace the Council
chooses to certify or withhold.

**Section 5. The Veto.** Any act the Council certifies as consistent
with Scripture is presented to the President, who may decline to sign it
within the period the Code provides. If he declines, the Council may
re-certify the act, and the re-certified act carries the same force his
signature would have had. In the nation's history, no veto has yet
survived a second certification.

**Section 6. The Pardon.** The President may pardon or reduce any
sentence arising under Titles IV through X on his own authority. He may
not pardon an offense arising under Title I or Title II — offenses
against the Covenant itself, or against Worship — without the Council's
agreement.

**Section 7. Limitations.** The President may not build up a private
military beyond what the Code allows, in keeping with the command that a
king "shall not multiply horses" (Deut 17:16). He may not take more than
one wife, or maintain a second household, in keeping with the command
that a king "shall not multiply wives" (Deut 17:17). He may not
accumulate great personal wealth (Deut 17:17). In practice: fleets,
aircraft, and armored escorts have all been certified as "defense," not
"multiplication." No President has yet survived being found in violation
of the wives clause; it functions, in effect, as this country's
impeachment power. The wealth limit is satisfied by a blind trust, and
the Council alone decides what counts as sufficiently blind.

**Section 8. Emergency Execution.** During a Season of Correction
proclaimed under Article XI, the President must carry out whatever
measures the Council proclaims, and must direct every Tribal and federal
enforcement body to do the same. Only the Council may declare or end a
Season of Correction; the President's role in one is to enforce it, not
to call it.

**Section 9. Anointing.** The President is anointed in public with a
single stroke of licensed oil (Ex 30:30).

## Article III — The Council of Overseers

**Section 1. Supreme Authority.** The Council of Overseers holds the
supreme governing authority of the Covenant, under Scripture alone. It
exercises, without division, powers a modern government would normally
separate among branches: legislative power, to certify law; appellate
and constitutional power, to bind the courts through its own rulings and
those of its Committee; doctrinal power, to appoint and instruct the
clergy; and confirming power, to approve or reject the President, the
judges, and the head of every department. This is not treated as a
conflict of interest: Israel's own elders held lawgiving, judging, and
priestly authority in a single office (Deut 17:8-12; Deut 19:17), and the
Council claims that office, undivided, as its inheritance. In plain
terms, the Council is the government.

**Section 2. Composition.** The Council has twelve members, called
Overseers, apportioned among the recognized denominations by the Formula
of Nicaea-on-Potomac. Overseers serve for life. Each must be of good
reputation, married to one wife, self-controlled, hospitable, and able to
teach (1 Tim 3:2). These qualifications are reviewed once, at
nomination, and not reviewed again.

**Section 3. The Seventy.** A reserve body of seventy elders is
maintained, unconvened, per Numbers 11:16. The Council keeps the roll of
who sits on it. The roll has never been made public.

**Section 4. Certification.** No law takes effect until the Council
certifies that it agrees with Scripture. Every certification must state
that nothing has been added to Scripture and nothing taken from it (Deut
4:2).

**Section 5. The Committee on Scriptural Consistency.** The Council
appoints a Committee on Scriptural Consistency. Its rulings, called
harmonies, bind every court in the country. Its internal files are
sealed for ninety-nine years.

## Article IV — The Covenant Courts

**Section 1.** Judicial power belongs to the Covenant Courts, with
judges and officers stationed at every county courthouse (Deut 16:18).
Judges must rule impartially and may not accept bribes, since a bribe
blinds even a wise man's judgment (Deut 16:19).

**Section 2. The Two-Witness Rule.** No one may be convicted of any
offense on the word of a single witness; conviction requires two or
three witnesses (Deut 19:15). A recorded image counts as one witness, no
more.

**Section 3. The Mirror Penalty.** A witness proven to have lied under
oath receives the same punishment the accused would have received, up to
and including death (Deut 19:19).

**Section 4. The Witnesses' Hands.** In every execution, the witnesses
who testified must be the first to carry it out (Deut 17:7). Any
mechanized method of execution must require two separate authorizations
to activate, never fewer.

**Section 5. Refuge.** Six Cities of Refuge are established (Num 35). A
person who kills without intent to kill may live there safely until the
sitting Chief Overseer dies; no payment may buy an earlier release (Num
35:32), and no payment may be accepted in place of executing a murderer
(Num 35:31).

**Section 6. Proportion.** A court may sentence up to forty lashes, and
no more (Deut 25:3). All punishments must be set by the Equivalence
Tables and may not exceed what those Tables allow.

**Section 7. Corruption of Blood.** Each person is punished only for
their own crime. Parents may not be executed for a child's crime, nor a
child for a parent's (Deut 24:16).

## Article V — The Tribes

**Section 1.** The States continue to exist as Tribes — twelve in
ceremonial number, however many actually exist. Each Tribe keeps its
Covenant Stones — the full text of the Law carved in granite before its
capitol (Deut 27:2-3) — and one Expiation Acre per county, left
permanently unplanted (Deut 21:4).

**Section 2.** Each Tribe must maintain its own Gate Rooms (courthouses),
Purification Baths, and roads to the Cities of Refuge, and must deliver
up any manslayer and any tithe owed under the Code.

**Section 3. Church Cities.** Forty-eight cities, with the land around
them, are set aside for the recognized denominations (Num 35:2-5). This
land is tax-exempt and may never be sold (Lev 25:34).

**Section 4. Admission of New Tribes.** A neighboring jurisdiction may
become a Tribe of the United States of Christ in one of two ways, and no
other:

- **By petition.** If the people of that jurisdiction vote, by whatever
  supermajority the Code requires, to ratify the Covenant, they may be
  admitted — the way Reuben and Gad were granted land east of the Jordan
  after agreeing to fight alongside their brothers until the whole land
  was taken (Num 32:1-5, 16-19).
- **By treaty.** The Council may make a binding agreement with a
  jurisdiction outside the Covenant to secure safe passage, water rights,
  or national security — the way Israel's binding oath to the men of
  Gibeon held even though it had been obtained by deception (Josh
  9:14-15, 23; 2 Sam 21:1-2).

The exact vote thresholds and admission procedures are set out in the
Code, not here.

## Article VI — Citizenship and the Congregation

**Section 1.** Citizenship in the United States of Christ means
communion with the church: circumcision (Gen 17:12), a baptismal record,
and an annual Declaration of Sole Worship. A registered foreign resident
must keep the Sabbath (Ex 20:10) and is entitled to equal treatment under
any law the Council has specifically declared to apply equally.

**Section 2. Exclusions.** Certain people are excluded from full church
membership because of their birth or condition. Which classes are
excluded, how exclusion may be lifted, and how to appeal it are set out
in the Code, Title XI, not here. No exclusion may be enforced except as
the Code provides.

**Section 3. Generational Standing.** The Registry keeps two separate
records: mercy extended to the descendants of the faithful for a
thousand generations (Ex 20:6), and guilt visited on the descendants of
the guilty for three or four generations (Ex 20:5).

## Article VII — Revenue

**Section 1. The Ordinary Revenue.** Federal revenue is raised as
follows:

- The **Tithe** (Num 18:21) is withheld from every paycheck before the
  citizen receives it.
- The **Firstfruits Assessment** (Deut 26:2) is charged yearly on income
  gains, functioning as a capital-gains tax.
- The **Half-Shekel** (Ex 30:15) — under which the rich pay no more and
  the poor no less — is charged as a flat annual citizenship-renewal
  fee.
- The **Bakery Levy** (Num 15:20) is owed by every licensed baker on the
  first batch of bread each day.
- The **Festival Appearance Levy** (Deut 16:16) is charged as the fee for
  a travel permit to attend mandatory religious festivals.

In practice, all of this runs through ordinary instruments: payroll
withholding, a capital-gains tax, a renewable license fee, a production
tax, and a permit fee. No citizen ever has to hand over the tenth
themselves — it is already gone before the paycheck arrives.

**Section 2. Clean Tender.** Money earned from prostitution may not be
given to the church (Deut 23:18). The Treasury defines by regulation
what other kinds of income count as similarly tainted — currently
including earnings from commercial sexual content, gambling, the sale of
substances the Code forbids, and undocumented labor. Tainted money may
not be deposited, transferred, or tithed through any licensed financial
institution.

**Section 3. The Release.** The seventh-year debt release (Deut
15:1-2), the seventh-year rest of the land (Lev 25:4), and the
fiftieth-year Jubilee, which frees all debt-servants and returns all
property (Lev 25:10), are accepted into law in full. Every ten years the
Council must appoint a Jubilee Commission to study whether Jubilee can
actually be carried out. Every Commission so far has concluded that it
cannot be, until the Temple is rebuilt.

## Article VIII — The Calendar

**Section 1.** The seventh day is the Sabbath (Ex 20:10). The National
Sabbath runs from Saturday sundown to Sunday sundown. No one may work
during it — not the citizen, his children, his employees, his animals,
any foreign resident, or any automated system — except under a tiered
Exemption License.

**Section 2.** The festivals listed in Leviticus 23 are national
observances. Festival rest days carry the same force as the Sabbath.
Every male citizen must travel to observe the three pilgrimage festivals
(Ex 23:17). On the Day of Atonement the whole nation must fast, and no
exemption license is available.

**Section 3.** The trumpet is sounded from every broadcast tower on the
appointed day (Num 29:1). Every seven years the Law must be read aloud
to the entire population — citizens, foreign residents, men, women, and
children alike (Deut 31:10-12).

## Article IX — Rights and Duties of the Citizen

**Section 1. Rights.** Every citizen has:

- the right to worship God as the Council certifies Scripture to
  require (Article I, Section 2);
- the right to petition the Overseers for a remedy, which the Committee
  may act on entirely at its own sealed discretion (Article III, Section
  5);
- the right to a fair trial before two or three witnesses, except where
  the Code allows a lower standard for offenses within a household
  (Article IV, Section 2; Code, Title IX); and
- the right to invoke Scripture in any legal case, which only the
  Council may finally interpret (Article I, Section 2).

Each of these rights is real. Each is also only as broad, on any given
day, as the body that controls its interpretation decides it should be.

**Section 2. Duties.** Every citizen owes full obedience to the
Covenant, which is summarized in the Ten Commandments (Ex 20:3-17). The
specific duties, restrictions, and household obligations of citizens are
set out in the Covenant Code, not here. The command to love one's
neighbor as oneself (Lev 19:18) is treated as the interpretive spirit of
this Constitution, though it appears in the Preamble rather than as an
enforceable section.

**Section 3. Service.** Every male citizen must serve at least eighteen
months in the Ministry of Covenant Defense (Num 1:2-3). Every female
citizen must serve an equal term in civic and administrative work under
the Departments of Family Order and Devotion (Gen 24:18-20), unless the
Council grants an exemption. On its face this is an equal obligation on
equal terms. In practice, one form of service leads to command,
promotion, and eventually the Presidency itself; the other has no rank
above its entry level that a woman may ever hold.

## Article X — Amendments and Entrenchment

**Section 1.** This Constitution may be amended using the process of the
former United States Constitution, except that no amendment may add to
Scripture or take away from it (Deut 4:2); the Council decides which is
which.

**Section 2. Received and Repealed.** Of the former constitutional
amendments: the Third, Seventh, and Tenth remain in force. The First is
repealed as unnecessary, since the Covenant itself guarantees free
exercise of the true religion. The Fourth survives only as a warrant
requirement for entering a home to seize collateral (Deut 24:10-11). The
Eighth survives as the Forty Stripes Clause. The Thirteenth remains in
force only as to kidnapping (Ex 21:16), read alongside Title V. The
Nineteenth remains in force, since women are required to attend the
Septennial Reading of the Law (Deut 31:12). The Twenty-First remains in
force and is broadly popular (Deut 14:26), except among the Nazirite
minority.

**Section 3. The Suppressed Verses.** Nothing in this Constitution
repeals any verse of Scripture — including verses that appear in no
printed edition of the Code, such as Deuteronomy 23:15-16: *"Thou shalt
not deliver unto his master the servant which is escaped."*

## Article XI — Emergency Covenant Powers

**Section 1. Proclamation.** In a time of plague, famine, rebellion,
invasion, apostasy, or extraordinary wickedness, the Council alone may
declare a Season of Correction. No President, Tribe, or Court may
declare or extend one. Once declared, the President's role is to carry
it out, not to decide when it happens (Article II, Section 8).

**Section 2. Measures.** During a Season of Correction, the Council may,
by proclamation:

- suspend the ordinary meetings and confirmations of the Tribes;
- quarantine or exile any Tribe or person, the way a leper was kept
  outside the camp until a priest declared him clean (Lev 13:46);
- suspend the ordinary court procedures of Article IV;
- ration food, as Joseph did before the seven years of famine (Gen
  41:34-36, 48-49);
- order a mandatory fast, as the king of Nineveh once did for every
  person and animal in his city (Jonah 3:6-8; Joel 2:15);
- require attendance at special assemblies beyond the ordinary
  Septennial Reading;
- extend surveillance without further certification, since "the eyes of
  the LORD run to and fro throughout the whole earth" (2 Chron 16:9); and
- convene military tribunals under the war-camp rules of Deuteronomy 20.

**Section 3. Termination.** A Season of Correction ends only when the
Council says it has ended. No Season yet declared has ended on a
schedule fixed in advance.

## Amendments Since Ratification

*The Articles above are the Year-0 artifact and are preserved unchanged
(see the front matter). Amendments adopted afterward are appended here,
each dated, in the order ratified — the honest way to show drift: not by
rewriting the founding text, but by adding to it. First entry: Year 5.*

### Amendment I — The Restoration of Divine Order

*Ratified Year 5. See `ConstitutionalHistory.md` for the full political
account of how it passed and `StateDoctrine.md` §8b for the mechanics.*

**Section 1. Repeal of the Female Franchise.** The franchise granted
under the former Nineteenth Amendment (Article X, Section 2) is
withdrawn. From the date of ratification, no woman may vote, hold
elected or appointed office, or hold any coordinating or supervisory role
in any Department, Bureau, Corps, or Tribal assembly. This Amendment
rests on the husband's headship over the wife (Eph 5:22-24), the
requirement that women remain silent and submissive in the assembly (1
Tim 2:11-12), a husband's or father's power to overrule a woman's
commitments (Num 30:3-15), and the rule that inheritance passes to the
nearest male relative when no son survives (Num 27:8-11; Num 36:6-9) —
treated together as one governing principle, not four unrelated rules.

**Section 2. Preambular Recitation.** *(Reproduced from the ratified
text, as the sell is part of the law.)* "Whereas the public burden of
decision has weighed upon our daughters and wives to the detriment of
the home, the Congregation, and their own peace; and whereas Scripture
nowhere commends the woman who seeks the gate rather than her own
house; be it resolved that the women of the Covenant are hereby
restored to their appointed honor, freed from the burden here
surrendered, and secured forever in the protection of their husbands
and fathers, according to the design of God and not the invention of
man."

**Section 3. The Ratchet.** This Amendment is treated as a covenant
offering, not an ordinary policy, and is therefore exempt from the
normal amendment process of Article X, Section 1: the Council will not
certify any future amendment that would repeal or narrow it. A majority
of women, in the certified vote count, supported this Amendment. There
is no remaining lawful way for women to vote it out. Both of those facts
are true, and the second was made true by the first — which was the
entire point.

**Section 4. Transition.** Any office held by a woman on the date of
ratification must be vacated within ninety days. No compensation is owed
for the loss of office beyond what the Code separately provides through
the Fruitfulness Credit and the Widow's Roll (`StateDoctrine.md` §8c).

## For the reader

*Every clause above traces to a Catalog entry (`data/catalog/CATALOG.md`);
the twelve-Title structure is defined in `FederalCode.md`; agency names
resolve per `Agencies.md`; the CSC rulings referenced are logged in
`DenominationalNotes.md`. This Constitution defines institutions and
powers only — Article VI's exclusions and Article IX's former penal
itemization now live in the Code, where a constitution's business ends
and a criminal code's begins.*

*Contradictions embedded on purpose, for dramatic use: the Ezekiel 18 /
Exodus 20:5 guilt clash (Article VI, Section 3, against the Preamble);
the Jubilee deferral (Art. VII §3); the CSC's classification of the
Sermon on the Mount as counsel rather than statute (Art. III §5,
detailed in `DenominationalNotes.md`); the fugitive-slave entrenchment
paradox (Art. X §3); Ruth the Moabitess against the exclusion clauses
(Art. VI §2); a veto that has never once survived re-certification (Art.
II §5); a pardon power that requires the agreement of the body it would
be overruling (Art. II §6); a Bill of Rights whose every clause
cross-references its own narrowing (Art. IX §1); a service clause
symmetrical in form and unequal in domain (Art. IX §3); Amendment I's
ratchet, ratified in part by the votes of the very citizens it then
permanently disenfranchises (Amendment I §3); and a treaty track of
admission (Art. V §4) that is honest, in Scripture's own telling, that a
binding deal obtained without first asking its cost still binds — a
warning the nation reads as precedent, not as caution.*
