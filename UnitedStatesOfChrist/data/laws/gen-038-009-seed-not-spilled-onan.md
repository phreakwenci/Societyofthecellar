# The Seed Not Spilled (Onan) — the Biological Sanctity Doctrine

> *And Onan knew that the seed should not be his; and it came to pass, when he went in unto his brother's wife, that he spilled it on the ground, lest that he should give seed to his brother. And the thing which he did displeased the LORD: wherefore he slew him also.*  
> — Genesis 38:9-10 (KJV)

| **Summary** | Onan, refusing to father a child for his dead brother, 'spilled his seed on the ground' and God killed him — the narrative from which a whole doctrine of the sanctity and proper destination of 'seed' is derived. |
| **Original Audience** | The patriarchal narrative's audience; the immediate issue is levirate duty (raising up seed for a dead brother), not contraception. |
| **Immediate Context** | Genesis 38, the Judah-and-Tamar story; Onan's sin is read variously as refusing levirate duty, as 'wasting seed,' or as fraud against Tamar. The text says only that he withheld seed and died for it. |
| **Historical Context** | For most of history 'Onanism' was read as the ban on any non-procreative emission — the basis of Catholic and much Protestant teaching against contraception and masturbation. Modern scholarship reads the sin as the *levirate refusal*, not the biology. The USOC needs the older, broader reading, because it wants a scriptural hook for regulating reproductive technology the Bible never imagined. |
| **Penalty** | Death, by God directly. |
| **Exceptions** | None offered by the regime; the levirate-duty reading (that the sin was refusing the brother, not the biology) is the dissident's argument. |
| **Cross References** | • Genesis 38:6-11<br>• Deuteronomy 25:5-10<br>• Leviticus 18:20<br>• Deuteronomy 23:2 |
| **Related NT Passages** | • 1 Corinthians 6:19-20 — 'your body is the temple of the Holy Ghost'; the proof-text the reproductive regime prefers to Onan itself<br>• Matthew 19:12 — 'eunuchs which have made themselves eunuchs for the kingdom' — an inconvenient counter-text |
| **Internal Contradictions** | • The plain sin is refusing levirate duty (Deut 25:5), not emission — yet the regime builds a biology doctrine on it. And Deuteronomy's levirate law *commands* a man to impregnate his brother's widow, which the USOC's own adultery and lineage laws would otherwise forbid. [Deuteronomy 25:5-10, Leviticus 18:16] |
| **SAB Categories** | — |
| **USofC Implementation** | The scriptural cornerstone of the Biological Sanctity Act. From 'the seed should not be his' the FPA derives: (1) every embryo is a life, so all IVF embryos are unlawful and 'to be destroyed unless divine circumstances intervene from the Authority'; (2) seed must reach its lawful destination within marriage, so IUI is permitted only with the husband's own seed — donor seed is construed as adultery and produces a lineage-illegitimate child (mamzer, Deut 23:2); (3) 'wasted seed' (contraception, sterilization) is a sanctity-score offense. The doctrine reaches technologies the text never imagined by reading Onan as biology, not duty. |
| **Agency** | Family Purity Administration (FPA) |
| **Constitutional Analogue** | The logical terminus of fetal-personhood law applied to reproductive medicine — cf. the 2024 Alabama frozen-embryo ruling, taken to its conclusion: embryos as persons, IVF as homicide, donor conception as adultery. |
| **Scene Ideas** | • A couple who conceived their older children by IVF before the Leave, now required to account for the embryos still frozen in a clinic — 'lives' the state says they must not let perish and must not implant.<br>• An FPA hearing on whether a child conceived by donor IUI is a citizen or a mamzer (Legitimacy Certification), the parents' marriage now evidence against them.<br>• A doctor destroying embryos under law while a chaplain reads Genesis 38 aloud. |
| **Satirical Uses** | • A doctrine against 'wasting seed' built on a story whose actual sin was a man refusing to do his duty — the regime mistakes the biology for the point, which is the regime in one verse. |
| **Bureaucratic Uses** | • Biological Sanctity Act embryo-disposition orders<br>• donor-conception Legitimacy Certification<br>• the 'divine circumstances' exemption petition to the Authority |
