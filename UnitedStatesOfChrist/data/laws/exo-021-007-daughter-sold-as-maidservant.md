# Daughter Sold as Maidservant; Redemption Rights

> *And if a man sell his daughter to be a maidservant, she shall not go out as the menservants do. If she please not her master, who hath betrothed her to himself, then shall he let her be redeemed: to sell her unto a strange nation he shall have no power, seeing he hath dealt deceitfully with her.*  
> — Exodus 21:7-8 (KJV)

| **Historical Context** | Sale of daughters in famine or debt is documented across the ANE (Nuzi contracts closely parallel this text's betrothal-servitude hybrid). The law's 'protections' — redemption, no foreign resale, maintenance — are real within the institution and damning of it: they regulate the sale of children rather than imagining its absence. |
| **Penalty** | Loss of resale rights for the deceitful master; redemption must be permitted. |
| **Exceptions** | Deut 15:17 later applies the release framework 'unto thy maidservant likewise', partially superseding the no-exit rule. |
