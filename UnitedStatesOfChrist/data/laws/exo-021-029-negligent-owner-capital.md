# The Known Goring Ox: Owner Dies, Unless Ransomed

> *But if the ox were wont to push with his horn in time past, and it hath been testified to his owner, and he hath not kept him in, but that he hath killed a man or a woman; the ox shall be stoned, and his owner also shall be put to death. If there be laid on him a sum of money, then he shall give for the ransom of his life whatsoever is laid upon him.*  
> — Exodus 21:29-30 (KJV)

| **Original Audience** | Israelite owners of dangerous animals, on notice. |
| **Immediate Context** | The escalation of v.28: notice plus negligence converts an accident into the owner's capital crime. This is the only capital offense in the Torah that money can commute (contrast Num 35:31 banning murder ransom). |
| **Historical Context** | The 'it hath been testified' clause invents the legal concept of notice: liability turns on what the owner knew. Hammurabi 251 fines the same negligence half a mina; the biblical version threatens death then sells mercy — a structure (capital exposure, monetary settlement) that functionally produces the ANE fine with extra terror. |
| **Penalty** | Death for the owner, commutable by assessed ransom; ox stoned regardless. |
| **Exceptions** | The ransom option itself — unique in biblical capital law. |
| **Cross References** | • Numbers 35:31 (murder ransom banned — the pointed contrast)<br>• Exodus 21:28<br>• Deuteronomy 22:8 (parapet law: build against foreseeable falls) |

| **SAB Categories** | • Absurdity<br>• Cruelty and Violence |
| **USofC Implementation** | The Attested Hazard doctrine: any death caused by a hazard formally attested to its owner (product recalls, safety citations, prior incidents) is capital negligence — commutable by Life Ransom, assessed by the court against the owner's net worth. Corporate officers carry personal Attestation Files; the Life Ransom actuarial market is the country's real stock exchange. |
