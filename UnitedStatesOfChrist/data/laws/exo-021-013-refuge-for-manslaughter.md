# A Place to Flee for the Unintentional Killer

> — Exodus 21:13 (KJV)

| **Summary** | Killing without premeditation ('God delivered him into his hand') is not capital; the killer flees to an appointed place of asylum. |
| **Original Audience** | Israelites navigating blood-vengeance culture: the avenger of blood was entitled to kill the killer unless asylum intervened. |
| **Immediate Context** | First intent-distinction in biblical law, grading v.12's absolute; Numbers 35 builds it into the six Cities of Refuge. |
| **Historical Context** | Asylum at altars was pan-Mediterranean; Israel institutionalizes it into designated cities with residency terms (until the high priest's death, Num 35:25 — history's strangest parole clock). The 'God delivered him' idiom makes accident a species of providence: the manslayer is God's instrument, hence protected. |
| **Penalty** | Exile to refuge in lieu of death; leaving early forfeits protection (Num 35:26-28). |
| **Exceptions** | Premeditation voids asylum (v.14). |

| **Internal Contradictions** | • 'God deliver him into his hand' makes the deity the proximate cause of accidents — a determinism the courts must administer without examining. [Numbers 35:22-25] |
| **SAB Categories** | — |
| **USofC Implementation** | The six federal Cities of Refuge, restored by statute: manslaughter defendants may physically outrun prosecution to a Refuge City and remain until the sitting Chief Overseer dies. The cities have become pressure-cooker municipalities of the almost-guilty — part sanctuary, part open-air prison, wholly surveilled, economically booming. |
| **Agency** | Covenant Courts |
| **Constitutional Analogue** | Sanctuary jurisdictions plus parole, keyed to an official's lifespan — a due-process fever dream. |
