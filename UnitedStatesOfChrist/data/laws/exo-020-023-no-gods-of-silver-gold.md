# No Gods of Silver or Gold

> *Ye shall not make with me gods of silver, neither shall ye make unto you gods of gold.*  
> — Exodus 20:23 (KJV)

| **Summary** | Restates the image ban with specificity: no manufacturing deities from precious metals, even as companions ('with me') to YHWH's worship. |
| **Original Audience** | Israel via Moses, immediately after the Decalogue's public delivery; the golden calf (Ex 32) will violate it exactly. |
| **Immediate Context** | Opens the 'Book of the Covenant' (Ex 20:22-23:33), the oldest law collection in the Bible; bridges the Decalogue to the altar law. |
| **Historical Context** | Precious-metal cult statues were temple treasury and god at once; melting the calf and making Israel drink it (Ex 32:20) follows an attested ANE pattern for annihilating a god. The 'with me' clause targets syncretism — adding gods alongside, not just replacing. |
| **Penalty** | None stated; the calf episode answers with plague and sword (Ex 32:27-28,35). |
| **Exceptions** | The tabernacle is nonetheless furnished with gold cherubim and gold everything (Ex 25) — sanctioned images in the sanctuary the law protects. |
| **Cross References** | • Exodus 32<br>• Exodus 20:4<br>• Deuteronomy 29:17<br>• Isaiah 31:7<br>• Hosea 8:4 |

| **SAB Categories** | — |
| **USofC Implementation** | Implemented as the Precious Metals Devotional Use Act: bullion ownership above a threshold requires an OIP affidavit that the metal is 'inert' (non-devotional). Gold religious statuary is contraband; gold on state regalia is 'ornament, not god.' The statute's real function is asset surveillance wearing a mitre. |
| **Agency** | Office of Idolatry Prevention (OIP) |
