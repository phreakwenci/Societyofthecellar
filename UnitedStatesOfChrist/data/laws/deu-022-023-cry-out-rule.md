# The City and the Field — the Cry-Out Rule and the Bride-Price Marriage

> *If a damsel that is a virgin be betrothed unto an husband, and a man find her in the city, and lie with her; Then ye shall bring them both out unto the gate of that city, and ye shall stone them with stones that they die; the damsel, because she cried not, being in the city; and the man, because he hath humbled his neighbour's wife... But if a man find a betrothed damsel in the field, and the man force her, and lie with her: then the man only that lay with her shall die: But unto the damsel thou shalt do nothing; there is in the damsel no sin worthy of death... For he found her in the field, and the betrothed damsel cried, and there was none to save her. If a man find a damsel that is a virgin, which is not betrothed, and lay hold on her, and lie with her, and they be found; Then the man that lay with her shall give unto the damsel's father fifty shekels of silver, and she shall be his wife; because he hath humbled her, he may not put her away all his days.*  
> — Deuteronomy 22:23-29 (KJV)

| **Summary** | Three linked cases graded entirely by geography and evidence, not by consent: a betrothed woman assaulted in the city is presumed complicit and stoned alongside her attacker because a city-dweller could theoretically be heard; the same woman assaulted in an open field is presumed innocent, because no one could have heard her cry for help; and an unbetrothed virgin who is 'found' with a man must be married to him for life, at a fixed bride-price, with no right of divorce ever granted him. |
| **Original Audience** | Israelite legal authorities administering betrothal and virginity law in an agrarian society where 'the city' and 'the field' were genuinely distinct zones of expected safety and isolation. |
| **Immediate Context** | Part of the Deuteronomic sexual-purity code (Deut 22:13-30), which opens with a bride's virginity being litigated by physical tokens (22:13-21) and closes with incest law (22:30). This passage is the Torah's only attempt at a consent standard, and it is entirely circumstantial: the presumption of guilt or innocence turns on where the act occurred, never on anything the woman herself did or said. |
| **Historical Context** | The city/field distinction is a real, and in its own terms almost humane, forensic innovation for its era — it explicitly rejects 'she must have wanted it' as the default and instead reasons from opportunity to cry for help, arriving at 22:26's remarkable sentence: 'there is in the damsel no sin worthy of death... even so is this matter [as when a man rises against his neighbor and kills him]' — an explicit analogy to being the victim of violent assault, not a participant in it. But the rule's mechanics still make a woman's exculpation depend entirely on unwitnessed geography, and the closing case (22:28-29) treats an unmarried victim's future as a transaction owed to her father, resolved by permanent marriage to her attacker rather than any relief to her. |
| **Penalty** | City case: both parties stoned. Field case: the man alone dies; the woman is explicitly cleared, 'there is... no sin worthy of death.' Unbetrothed case: fifty shekels to the father and mandatory, permanent marriage, with the perpetual-marriage clause functioning as the woman's only stated 'remedy.' |
| **Exceptions** | The field case is itself the exception the passage carves out of a harsher default; nothing further modifies it in the immediate text. |
| **Cross References** | • Deuteronomy 22:13-21<br>• Exodus 22:16-17<br>• Numbers 5:11-31<br>• Deuteronomy 21:14 |
| **Related NT Passages** | • John 8:3-11 — Jesus refuses to apply a comparable presumption of guilt to a woman 'taken in adultery,' regardless of city or field<br>• Matthew 1:19 — Joseph, believing Mary pregnant by another, resolves to act 'not willing to make her a publick example' — a quiet reversal of the shaming logic this statute assumes |
| **Internal Contradictions** | • The passage's own logic (22:26-27) explicitly clears a woman assaulted where no help could reach her — precisely the situation of a remote or isolated setting — yet institutions built on this same Code routinely process isolated-setting assault victims as complicit anyway, ignoring the one place their own inerrant text draws an unambiguous line in their favor. [Deuteronomy 22:26-27]<br>• The unbetrothed case's 'remedy' (22:28-29) is lifelong marriage to the assailant with no right of divorce ever granted him — framed as protection for the woman's economic future, it forecloses her own consent to marriage a second time, this time permanently, as the price of the first violation. [Deuteronomy 22:29, Exodus 22:16-17] |
| **SAB Categories** | • Injustice<br>• Misogyny |
| **USofC Implementation** | The Family Purity Administration's founding text for adjudicating assault claims: the 'city or field' inquiry is preserved almost verbatim as the first question in any Title IV sexual-assault intake — was the location one where a cry for help could plausibly have been heard? A finding of 'field' (isolation) formally clears the victim of any presumption of complicity, exactly as the statute requires. In practice, re-education camps and holding facilities are consistently found, on inspection, to satisfy the 'field' standard — remote, unheard, no one to save her — which makes the FPA's own doctrine, honestly applied, an acquittal for every victim assaulted in state custody. It is not honestly applied. Custody cases are administratively routed instead through the sotah/jealousy framework (`num-005-011-jealousy-ordeal-sotah`) or reclassified as a purity matter arising from the original offense that led to her detention, so the geographic exculpation the state's own law guarantees her is never actually reached. The unbetrothed marriage remedy (22:28-29) survives as the **Restitution Marriage** option in Family Order law — rarely invoked, mostly by families seeking to avoid the shame of an unmarried pregnancy, and never available to a woman already in state custody, since the camp itself has no father to whom fifty shekels might be owed. |
| **Agency** | Family Purity Administration (FPA) |
| **Constitutional Analogue** | The closest biblical approach to a corroboration-based consent standard, and — read honestly — more protective of an isolated-setting victim than the regime that claims this text as inerrant law actually is; a real-world echo of jurisdictions where a victim's 'opportunity to resist or flee' still perversely shapes prosecutorial charging decisions. |
| **Scene Ideas** | • An FPA intake officer running the 'city or field' questionnaire on Laura's case, checking every box the statute requires for exculpation, and then watching a supervisor reclassify the file anyway.<br>• A defense counsel reading Deuteronomy 22:26-27 aloud in full, slowly, in a room where everyone already knows it will change nothing.<br>• A Restitution Marriage broker's office, quietly doing a brisk business in families who would rather marry a daughter to her assailant than have the assault on record at all. |
| **Satirical Uses** | • The regime's own foundational sexual-assault statute contains a clean, geography-based acquittal for exactly Laura's situation, and administers her case as though the verse does not exist — because the verse that would clear her was never the one anyone intended to enforce. |
| **Bureaucratic Uses** | • The City/Field Intake Determination (Form FPA-2)<br>• Restitution Marriage brokerage licensing<br>• the administrative reclassification memo that moves a 'field'-cleared case into sotah/jealousy jurisdiction instead |
