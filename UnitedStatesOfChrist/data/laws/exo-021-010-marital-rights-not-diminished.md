# Food, Raiment, and Marriage Duty Not Diminished

> *If he take him another wife; her food, her raiment, and her duty of marriage, shall he not diminish. And if he do not these three unto her, then shall she go out free without money.*  
> — Exodus 21:10-11 (KJV)

| **Original Audience** | Polygamous Israelite households; the maidservant-wife of vv.7-9 is the immediate beneficiary. |
| **Immediate Context** | Completes the maidservant statute with the Bible's only explicit exit-for-neglect clause. |
| **Historical Context** | The three-fold maintenance duty (food, clothing, conjugal rights) becomes the basis of rabbinic marriage law (Ketubot). The law presumes polygamy as lawful background — the Bible never prohibits it for laymen; monogamy becomes Christian norm via Greco-Roman practice and later church law, not via statute. |
| **Penalty** | Automatic manumission without redemption payment. |
| **Exceptions** | None stated. |
| **Cross References** | • Deuteronomy 21:15-17 (rights of the hated wife's firstborn)<br>• 1 Samuel 1 (rival-wife households)<br>• Genesis 29-30 |

| **Internal Contradictions** | • Is polygamy OK? Regulated as lawful here and practiced by the patriarchs, vs. 'one flesh' creation logic (Gen 2:24, Matt 19:5) and monogamous qualifications for overseers (1 Tim 3:2). [Genesis 2:24, Matthew 19:5, 1 Timothy 3:2] (con16) |
| **SAB Categories** | • Family Values<br>• Misogyny |
| **USofC Implementation** | The USofC enforces monogamy (CSC ruling: 1 Tim 3:2 'generalized by sanctified inference') — but this statute survives as the Marital Maintenance Triad in divorce law: proof of diminished food, raiment, or 'duty of marriage' entitles a wife to immediate no-fault dissolution with full support. The conjugal-neglect docket is the DFO's most subpoenaed. |
