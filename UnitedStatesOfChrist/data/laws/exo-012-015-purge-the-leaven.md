# Put Away Leaven; Eat Unleavened Bread (the Purge)

> *Seven days shall ye eat unleavened bread; even the first day ye shall put away leaven out of your houses: for whosoever eateth leavened bread from the first day until the seventh day, that soul shall be cut off from Israel.*  
> — Exodus 12:15 (KJV)

| **Summary** | During the seven days of Unleavened Bread, all leaven must be purged from the house and only unleavened bread eaten; eating leavened bread means being 'cut off from Israel.' |
| **Original Audience** | Israel at the first Passover and every Passover after; the leaven purge memorializes the haste of the Exodus (no time for bread to rise). |
| **Immediate Context** | The Passover institution (Ex 12); the leaven ban runs the seven-day feast, with a household search for leaven that became a formal rite. |
| **Historical Context** | Leaven became a standing metaphor for corruption and pride (1 Cor 5:6-8, 'a little leaven leaveneth the whole lump'; Jesus's 'leaven of the Pharisees'). The household search for leaven (bedikat chametz) is one of the most domestic, intimate rituals in the tradition — a family, by candlelight, hunting crumbs. |
| **Penalty** | Being 'cut off from Israel' — the same karet exclusion as the uncircumcised. |
| **Exceptions** | None during the feast. Outside the holy week, leavened bread is ordinary and permitted. |
| **Cross References** | • Exodus 13:6-7<br>• Deuteronomy 16:3-4<br>• Leviticus 23:6<br>• 1 Corinthians 5:6-8 |
| **Related NT Passages** | • 1 Corinthians 5:6-8 — 'purge out therefore the old leaven'; the feast spiritualized into moral purity<br>• Luke 12:1 — 'beware ye of the leaven of the Pharisees, which is hypocrisy'<br>• Matthew 13:33 — leaven as the kingdom (a rare positive), an inconvenient counter-image |
| **Internal Contradictions** | • Leaven is 'cut off'-level corruption during the holy week yet the image of the kingdom itself in Matthew 13:33 — the same substance is damnation and the reign of God depending on the page. [Matthew 13:33, 1 Corinthians 5:7] |
| **SAB Categories** | — |
| **USofC Implementation** | The keystone of the Bread Laws. During the holy weeks the Bureau of Daily Bread issues only government-milled unleavened flour; leaven, honey, herbs, and private sourdough starters are contraband (a 'symbol of impurity and disobedience'), and households undergo — or self-perform under threat of inspection — the Search for Leaven. Eating leavened bread in the holy week is a 'cutting off' offense: sanctity-score collapse, ration suspension, and, aggravated, re-education. The domestic candlelight search becomes a surveillance ritual: a family hunting its own crumbs while the Manna watches. |
| **Agency** | Bureau of Daily Bread (BDB) |
| **Constitutional Analogue** | Sumptuary and dietary law enforced into the home; the household search is a warrantless inspection normalized as devotion. |
| **Scene Ideas** | • The father bakes his late wife's old cookies in Year 10 and is reported for the unleavened-week violation — the tenderest act in the play caught by the harshest, smallest law. (This is the play's climax; the cookies and the smuggled letters are found together.)<br>• The family's annual Search for Leaven, once warm, now performed knowing a neighbor or a child may report what the candle finds.<br>• A black-market sourdough starter passed hand to hand like samizdat. |
| **Satirical Uses** | • A regime that calls leaven the image of the kingdom of God for fifty-one weeks makes eating it a 'cutting off' crime for one. |
| **Bureaucratic Uses** | • holy-week unleavened-flour issuance (BDB)<br>• the Search for Leaven (household self-inspection)<br>• leaven-contraband citations |
