# The Goring Ox: Stone the Ox, Owner Quit

> *If an ox gore a man or a woman, that they die: then the ox shall be surely stoned, and his flesh shall not be eaten; but the owner of the ox shall be quit... Whether he have gored a son, or have gored a daughter, according to this judgment shall it be done unto him.*  
> — Exodus 21:28,31 (KJV)

| **Summary** | An ox that kills a person is stoned and its meat is taboo; the owner of a first-offense ox bears no liability. The rule applies equally when the victim is a child. |
| **Original Audience** | Israelite livestock owners; the ox is the tractor, truck, and 401(k) of the household. |
| **Immediate Context** | Opens the famous goring-ox series (vv.28-32), the comparative-law showpiece of the chapter. |
| **Historical Context** | Laws of Eshnunna 53-55 and Hammurabi 250-252 handle the same ox with fines only; Israel alone executes the animal and bans its meat — the ox has shed human blood (Gen 9:5 holds beasts accountable) and is contaminated by it. Legal historians live on this pericope: it shows liability, foreseeability, and even animal culpability emerging in one barnyard. |
| **Penalty** | Stoning of the ox; flesh unusable; owner exempt (first offense). |
| **Exceptions** | A known goring ox changes everything (v.29); slave victims convert to a fixed fee (v.32). |
| **Cross References** | • Genesis 9:5<br>• Exodus 21:29-32<br>• Laws of Eshnunna 53-55 (comparative) |
| **Related NT Passages** | • 1 Corinthians 9:9 / 1 Timothy 5:18 — 'thou shalt not muzzle the ox': Paul's ox-law hermeneutics, allegorizing barnyard statutes for clergy pay |
| **Internal Contradictions** | — |
| **SAB Categories** | • Absurdity<br>• Cruelty and Violence |
| **USofC Implementation** | Extended by regulation to 'any instrumentality of death' — the Instrumentality Destruction Rule: the car, the gun, the robot arm that kills is ceremonially destroyed ('stoned') and cannot be salvaged or resold. Auto manufacturers lobbied for a firmware exception and lost, publicly, once — the regime's proudest consumer-protection story. |
| **Agency** | Restitution Administration (RA) |
| **Constitutional Analogue** | Civil asset forfeiture inverted (deodand revival — English law's forfeiture of death-causing objects, abolished 1846, resurrected here). |
