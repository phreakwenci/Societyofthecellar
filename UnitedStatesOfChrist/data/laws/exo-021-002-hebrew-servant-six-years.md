# Hebrew Servant Freed in the Seventh Year

> *If thou buy an Hebrew servant, six years he shall serve: and in the seventh he shall go out free for nothing. If he came in by himself, he shall go out by himself: if he were married, then his wife shall go out with him.*  
> — Exodus 21:2-3 (KJV)

| **Historical Context** | Hammurabi 117 releases the debt-slave after three years; the biblical term doubles it. Deut 15:12-15 adds mandatory severance gifts; Jer 34 shows the release being flouted and re-imposed. This applies to Hebrew men only — Lev 25:44-46 permits permanent chattel slavery of foreigners, the distinction on which every later pro-slavery sermon leaned. |
| **Penalty** | None stated for violation; Jeremiah 34:17 has God sentence release-violators to 'liberty... to the sword.' |
| **Exceptions** | The servant may elect permanent service (vv.5-6); female servants sold by fathers follow different rules (vv.7-11); foreigners are excluded from release entirely (Lev 25:44-46). |

| **Internal Contradictions** | • Does God approve of slavery? Regulated here and permitted permanently for foreigners (Lev 25:44-46), vs. 'ye were strangers in Egypt' ethics and Exodus's own liberation narrative. [Leviticus 25:44-46, Exodus 22:21, Deuteronomy 23:15-16] (con42) |
