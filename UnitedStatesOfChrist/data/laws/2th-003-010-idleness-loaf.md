# If Any Would Not Work, Neither Should He Eat

> *For even when we were with you, this we commanded you, that if any would not work, neither should he eat.*  
> — 2 Thessalonians 3:10 (KJV)

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| **ID** | 2th-003-010-idleness-loaf |
| **Citation** | 2 Thessalonians 3:10 |
| **Book** | 2 Thessalonians |
| **Chapter** | 3 |
| **Verses** | 10 |
| **Testament** | New |
| **Categories** | • Economic<br>• Civil<br>• Moral |
| **Law Type** | governmental_principle |
| **Summary** | Paul's rule for the Thessalonian church, addressed to believers who had stopped working while waiting on the Lord's imminent return: a person unwilling to work forfeits the community's provision, full stop. |
| **Original Audience** | The church at Thessalonica, some of whose members had abandoned ordinary labor, apparently convinced Christ's return was so near that work was pointless (2 Thess 2:1-2, 3:11's 'working not at all, but are busybodies'). |
| **Immediate Context** | Closes Paul's second letter to the Thessalonians, following his own example of laboring 'night and day' rather than eating anyone's bread for nought (3:8), and preceding a direct instruction to 'withdraw' from any brother who walks disorderly and will not work (3:6, 3:14). |
| **Historical Context** | A pastoral, congregation-specific rule addressed to a narrow problem (eschatological idleness), not a general economic theory; nothing in the passage contemplates a state administering bread rations, only a local church declining to subsidize members who had stopped contributing to a shared table. |
| **Penalty** | Withdrawal of provision ('neither should he eat'); the wider passage also counsels social withdrawal from the unrepentant idle (3:6, 3:14), tempered by 3:15's instruction not to count him as an enemy, but admonish him as a brother. |
| **Exceptions** | The rule targets the unwilling ('would not work'), not the unable — nothing in the text extends it to the disabled, the sick, the very old or young, or those without available work; 3:15 explicitly softens even the sanctioned case toward restoration, not permanent exclusion. |
| **Cross References** | • 1 Timothy 5:8<br>• Proverbs 6:6-11<br>• Ephesians 4:28<br>• 2 Thessalonians 3:6-15 |
| **Related NT Passages** | • 2 Thessalonians 3:6 — 'withdraw yourselves from every brother that walketh disorderly'<br>• 2 Thessalonians 3:15 — 'count him not as an enemy, but admonish him as a brother' |
| **Internal Contradictions** | • The passage's own closing instruction (3:15) treats the idle brother as someone to be restored through admonishment, not permanently deprived or exiled — a corrective, relational remedy that a state rationing bureau administering the same verse as a hard eligibility gate does not preserve. [2 Thessalonians 3:15]<br>• The rule is textually addressed to the voluntarily idle awaiting an imminent Second Coming, not to anyone unable to secure work in a labor market the state itself controls through licensing and registration — a distinction the ration system does not draw. [2 Thessalonians 3:11] |
| **SAB Categories** | • Injustice |
| **USofC Implementation** | The scriptural warrant for the Bread and Devotion Bureau's 'Idleness Loaf' policy: only citizens registered as employed, conscripted, or otherwise 'in service' (military, Rebekah Corps, licensed clergy, or a recognized household headship role) may draw a daily bread ration. An unregistered citizen — one between jobs, informally blacklisted, or simply not yet processed into the labor registry — draws no ration at all, regardless of ability or willingness to work, since 'registered' rather than 'willing' is the administrable proxy the BDB actually enforces. The gap between Paul's narrow pastoral rule (aimed at a handful of eschatologically-idle believers, softened by 3:15's instruction to admonish rather than exile) and a nationwide, no-exceptions ration gate is the satire: the verse never contemplated a state, let alone one with the power to make 'unregistered' synonymous with 'unwilling.' |
| **Agency** | Bread and Devotion Bureau (BDB) |
| **Constitutional Analogue** | A means-tested welfare eligibility requirement (e.g. work requirements attached to food assistance), stripped of any administrative appeal and enforced through a state-controlled labor registry rather than a private employer. |
| **Scene Ideas** | • A citizen recently widowed or between assignments, denied her ration at the counter because her registry status hasn't yet updated, while the clerk recites 3:15's 'admonish him as a brother' from a wall poster over her head.<br>• A black-market bread economy among the unregistered, run informally by exactly the kind of person 3:10 was written to discourage. |
| **Satirical Uses** | • A one-verse pastoral corrective to a small end-times cult inside a single congregation, scaled into the eligibility gate for a nation's bread supply. |
| **Bureaucratic Uses** | • BDB Form ID-1: Registered Service Verification<br>• Idleness Loaf denial notice<br>• Labor Registry status appeal (nominal; rarely granted) |
