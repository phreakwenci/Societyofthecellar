# Women Who Change the Natural Use (the Extension to Female Conduct)

> *For this cause God gave them up unto vile affections: for even their women did change the natural use into that which is against nature: And likewise also the men, leaving the natural use of the woman, burned in their lust one toward another; men with men working that which is unseemly, and receiving in themselves that recompence of their error which was meet.*  
> — Romans 1:26-27 (KJV)

| **Original Audience** | Paul's letter to the Roman church, part of an argument (Rom 1:18-32) that Gentile idolatry led God to 'give them up' to a catalogue of vices, of which this is one item among many (including envy, murder, disobedience to parents, and 'unmerciful' — the list is long and its selective modern quotation is itself a live theological controversy). |
| **Immediate Context** | The rhetorical setup for Romans 2, where Paul turns the accusation on the reader ('thou art inexcusable, O man, whosoever thou art that judgest') — a structure many scholars argue is a trap: build outrage at Gentile vice, then indict the judging reader for the same self-righteousness. The USOC's use of v.26-27 alone, detached from ch. 2's turn, is a live and contested reading. |
| **Historical Context** | Leviticus 18:22 and 20:13 address only male-male conduct; nothing in the Torah's plain text addresses female same-sex conduct at all. Romans 1:26 is therefore the historical and doctrinal hinge by which later Christian tradition extended the Levitical prohibition to women — a genuine act of textual extension, not a straightforward restatement, since the underlying Greek and the passage's own logic (paired with v.27's 'likewise also') has been read variously as female same-sex conduct, non-procreative heterosexual conduct, or ritual/cultic practice tied to idol worship, by different traditions across history. |
| **Penalty** | None stated directly in the text; it is descriptive ('God gave them up'), not a civil statute prescribing a human penalty — unlike Leviticus 20:13's explicit capital sentence for the male case. |
| **Exceptions** | None stated. The passage's function as a general argument about Gentile idolatry (not a freestanding civil code) is itself the strongest ground for reading it narrowly. |
| **Cross References** | • Leviticus 18:22<br>• Leviticus 20:13<br>• Romans 2:1<br>• 1 Corinthians 6:9-10 |
| **Related NT Passages** | • Romans 2:1 — 'thou art inexcusable, O man, whosoever thou art that judgest' — the passage's own immediate rebuttal to using it as a judgment tool<br>• 1 Corinthians 6:9-11 — a similar vice list ending 'and such were some of you: but ye are washed... but ye are justified,' emphasizing transformation over prosecution |
| **Internal Contradictions** | • The USOC prosecutes female same-sex conduct as a capital-adjacent Title IV offense by extending Romans 1:26 to carry Leviticus 20:13's civil death penalty — but Romans 1:26 states no penalty at all, and its very next verse (Rom 2:1) forbids the reader from using the list to judge another. The statute's own textual chapter break argues against its use as one. [Romans 2:1, Leviticus 20:13] |
| **SAB Categories** | — |
| **USofC Implementation** | The textual basis by which the Family Purity Administration extends Leviticus 18:22/20:13's male-only prohibition to female same-sex conduct, since the Torah is silent on it directly. In USOC doctrine, Romans 1:26 is read not as Paul's own argument (which turns against judgment in the very next verse) but as freestanding civil law, imported into Title IV alongside the Levitical statute and enforced with the same severity — in practice, referral to re-education rather than capital sentence, per the Code's general pattern of administrative commutation. This extension is precisely how two minors — one no older than the other — can be charged for a single kiss with no Torah-text naming their conduct at all. |
| **Agency** | Family Purity Administration (FPA) |
| **Constitutional Analogue** | The interpretive-extension problem real anti-sodomy statutes faced pre-Lawrence v. Texas when applied to conduct the original text never named; here compounded by the extension running through a passage whose own literary structure argues against being used that way. |
| **Scene Ideas** | • A defense counsel reading Romans 2:1 aloud in a juvenile tribunal and being overruled on the ground that the CSC has already certified the extension as settled.<br>• A Sunday-school teacher who has read the whole chapter, not just the two verses, quietly leaving the pulpit-quotation program.<br>• Two teenage girls' first kiss processed under a legal theory neither the Torah nor, arguably, Paul himself intended to supply. |
| **Satirical Uses** | • The regime builds a capital-adjacent statute on a passage whose next sentence forbids the reader from doing exactly that, and has certified the omission as doctrine. |
