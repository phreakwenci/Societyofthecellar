# Honor Father and Mother

> *Honour thy father and thy mother: that thy days may be long upon the land which the LORD thy God giveth thee.*  
> — Exodus 20:12 (KJV)

| **Citation** | Exodus 20:12 |
| **Book** | Exodus |

| **Testament** | Old |
| **Categories** | • Family<br>• Moral |
| **Law Type** | command |
| **Summary** | Positive duty of honor toward both parents, with a stated reward: long tenure in the land. The only commandment with an attached promise. |
| **Original Audience** | Primarily adult Israelites responsible for aging parents' maintenance, not small children being told to obey. |
| **Immediate Context** | Fifth commandment, opening the second tablet's social duties; enforced elsewhere by capital statutes against striking or cursing parents (Ex 21:15,17). |
| **Historical Context** | In a society without pensions, 'honor' meant material support of the old. Wisdom literature and ANE instruction texts (e.g., Egyptian Instruction of Any) carry the same duty. Jesus attacks the corban loophole by which dedicated funds escaped the obligation (Mark 7:9-13). |
| **Penalty** | None here (reward stated instead); striking or cursing a parent is death (Ex 21:15,17); the incorrigible son is stoned (Deut 21:18-21). |
| **Exceptions** | Jesus repeatedly relativizes family duty toward himself (Matt 10:35-37, Luke 14:26). |
| **Cross References** | • Exodus 21:15<br>• Exodus 21:17<br>• Leviticus 19:3<br>• Deuteronomy 21:18-21<br>• Proverbs 30:17 |
| **Related NT Passages** | • Ephesians 6:1-3 — Paul quotes it as 'the first commandment with promise'<br>• Mark 7:9-13 — Jesus condemns the corban loophole<br>• Matthew 10:35-37 — 'he that loveth father or mother more than me is not worthy of me'<br>• Luke 14:26 — 'if any man... hate not his father, and mother... he cannot be my disciple' |
| **Internal Contradictions** | • How should parents be treated? Honor them (here) vs. Jesus requiring disciples to 'hate' father and mother. [Luke 14:26, Matthew 10:35-37] (con107)<br>• Is it OK to call your father 'father'? Matthew 23:9 says call no man father — awkward for the commandment's wording. [Matthew 23:9] (con108) |
| **SAB Categories** | • Good Stuff |
| **USofC Implementation** | The Filial Piety Act: adult children file annual Parental Maintenance Statements; support is payroll-deducted like a second social security aimed backward. Public dishonor of a parent (defined by regulation, 40 pages) is a civil infraction ladder that tops out, theoretically, at the Incorrigibility Statute — never yet invoked, always available. |
| **Agency** | Department of Family Order (DFO) |
| **Constitutional Analogue** | Filial responsibility laws exist in ~30 US states, rarely enforced; the USofC enforces them universally and criminally. |
| **Scene Ideas** | • An estranged daughter's maintenance garnishment funding the abusive father she fled.<br>• A DFO mediator processing a corban-style trust — funds 'dedicated to the Church' to starve out a parent — with Mark 7 read aloud by opposing counsel.<br>• The first prosecutor to actually file under the Incorrigibility Statute becoming a national crisis. |
| **Satirical Uses** | • The promise clause is administered literally: verified honorers receive an actuarial bonus called Longevity Credit, paid, with no irony, from the estates of the dishonoring. |
| **Bureaucratic Uses** | • Annual Parental Maintenance Statement (Form F-5)<br>• Dishonor infraction schedule with point system<br>• Longevity Credit actuarial tables |
