# The Name Not in Vain

> *Thou shalt not take the name of the LORD thy God in vain; for the LORD will not hold him guiltless that taketh his name in vain.*  
> — Exodus 20:7 (KJV)

| **Summary** | Forbids empty, deceitful, or profane use of God's name — in its original setting, chiefly false oaths sworn by YHWH. |
| **Original Audience** | Israelites for whom oaths by the divine name were the backbone of contracts, testimony, and treaties. |
| **Immediate Context** | Third commandment; guards the covenant name revealed in Ex 3:14 and invoked in every legal proceeding. |
| **Historical Context** | In a world without notaries, the divine name was the enforcement mechanism of commerce: swearing falsely 'in the name' weaponized the god against the innocent party. Later Jewish practice fenced the law by never pronouncing the Name at all. Leviticus 24:16 sets stoning for blaspheming the Name. |
| **Penalty** | 'The LORD will not hold him guiltless' — divine sanction; blasphemy earns death by stoning in Lev 24:16. |
| **Exceptions** | Lawful oaths in God's name are commanded, not merely permitted (Deut 6:13, 10:20). |
| **Cross References** | • Leviticus 24:10-16<br>• Leviticus 19:12<br>• Deuteronomy 5:11<br>• Deuteronomy 6:13 |
| **Related NT Passages** | • Matthew 5:33-37 — Jesus forbids oaths entirely: 'Swear not at all'<br>• James 5:12 — repeats the total oath ban<br>• Matthew 26:63-64 — Jesus answers under oath before the high priest |
| **Internal Contradictions** | • Deuteronomy commands swearing by God's name; Jesus and James forbid all swearing — the USofC courtroom oath is scripturally impossible either way. [Deuteronomy 6:13, Matthew 5:34, James 5:12] |
| **SAB Categories** | — |
| **USofC Implementation** | The Sacred Speech Act. The SSC maintains the Register of Permitted Exclamations; broadcast media run a two-second Name-delay. Perjury on a Covenant oath is prosecuted as blasphemy, not mere false statement. The Matthew 5 'swear not at all' problem was resolved by CSC ruling: courtroom oaths are 'covenant affirmations,' not swearing. |
| **Agency** | Sacred Speech Commission (SSC) |
| **Constitutional Analogue** | A national blasphemy law; annuls First Amendment speech protection and the Art. VI option to affirm rather than swear. |
| **Scene Ideas** | • A live-broadcast engineer's finger hovering over the Name-delay button during a senator's unraveling speech.<br>• An SSC linguist testifying whether 'gosh' is a euphemistic misdemeanor or protected folk speech.<br>• A Quaker-descended defendant citing Matthew 5:34 to refuse the oath — and being charged under the very statute the oath serves. |
| **Satirical Uses** | • The state takes the Name in vain constantly — printing it on currency, campaign ads, and missile casings — because official use is by definition not 'vain.' |
